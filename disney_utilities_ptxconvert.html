ptxConvert - Images to Textures/Cucoloris

Converting in a terminal

Navigate to the image directory and use ptxconvert [source].[file_format] [destination].ptx

Note: To convert an image successfully to a ptx it needs to have a 'power of 2' resolution (ie: 256x256, 512x512, 2048x2048, etc.)

Example:

ptxconvert myfile.exr myfile.ptx

Supported file formats are:

  • .tif, .jpg, .png, etc. - standard image files
  • .tx - prman (tif) texture
  • .ptx - ptex texture (single face only)
  • .penv - ptex environment cube map

Modes

In tools like ptxconvert (part of the Ptex ecosystem used for converting image data, environment maps, or projections into Ptex format), edge or wrap modes dictate how pixel data behaves at the borders of faces or when mapping source images (like cube maps) onto geometry.

The modes you noticed handle out-of-bounds sampling and border transitions in the following ways:

1. Clamp

  • What it does: Extends the outermost edge pixels of the texture infinitely outward.
  • When it's used: Ideal for preventing ugly color bleeding, dark halos, or harsh lines at the edges of faces. If a sample point falls slightly outside the standard 0-to-1 coordinate space, it grabs the exact color of the closest border pixel.

2. Black

  • What it does: Fills any empty space or out-of-bounds areas with solid black (zero values).
  • When it's used: Helpful when dealing with non-tiling projections or when you want empty regions to default to zero/transparent rather than stretching or repeating texture details.

3. Periodic (or Repeat / Wrap)

  • What it does: Repeats or tiles the texture seamlessly across boundaries. If coordinates go past 1.0, the texture loops back to 0.0.
  • When it's used: Essential for repeating patterns (like bricks, fabrics, or tiles) where you want the texture to loop continuously across multiple face boundaries without a hard cutoff.

*(Depending on the specific build or version of the utility, you might also see modes like Reflection/Mirror, which mirrors the texture back on itself instead of repeating linearly).*

And to convert to each mode:

Clamp (default behavior):

ptxconvert myfile.ptx myfilewater.tif
ptxconvert -mode clamp myfile.tif myfile.ptx

Black:

ptxconvert -mode black myfile.tif myfile.ptx

Periodic:

ptxconvert -mode periodic myfile.tif myfile.ptx
