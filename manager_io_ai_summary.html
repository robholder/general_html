Manager.io Rental Property Configuration

Thorough Setup Instructions

This guide outlines the complete setup and operational workflows for managing multiple rental properties using custom Control Accounts, Groups, Divisions, and imported bank statements in the latest version of Manager.io.

Core Organization Layout

To isolate property performance while keeping unified corporate statements, the system maps financial data across two distinct dimensions: rows (Chart of Accounts / Groups) and columns (Divisions).

1. Divisions (Properties)

Divisions act as property tracking codes, allowing you to filter Profit & Loss statements by individual addresses.

  • Create a unique Division for each rental property (e.g., Sawtooth, Silverbell, and General) under Settings > Divisions.
  • The "General" Division Strategy: Always create an explicit division named General / Shared for corporate expenses (like postage, CPA fees, or software). This keeps shared overhead from distorting individual property yields and prevents these lines from cluttering the audit logs.
  • Tag the Division field on every single transaction line item (invoices, payments, receipts) to ensure precise asset, liability, and expense tracking.

2. Control Accounts & Sub-Ledger Modules

Control Accounts act as summary containers on the Balance Sheet to group individual sub-accounts into clean, single-line items.

Step 1: Create the "Property Escrow Buckets" (Mortgage Escrow) Control Account (Assets)

  1. Go to Settings > Control Accounts.
  2. Select Bank and Cash Accounts
  3. Click the New Control Account button.
  4. Configure the form:
    • Name: Mortgage Escrow
    • Type: Select Asset.
  5. Click Create.

Step 2: Create and Link Real Escrow Cash Accounts

Escrow accounts hold cash belonging to you in trust. They are assets and must be created as Cash Accounts so they can receive multi-line statement file imports.

  1. Go to the Bank and Cash Accounts tab.
  2. Click New Bank or Cash Account.
  3. Build the separate property accounts: e.g.,
    • Mortgage Escrow Account - Sawtooth and Mortgage Escrow Account - Silverbell
    • Control Account: Select Mortgage Escrow.
    • Division: Select the corresponding property division (e.g., Sawtooth and Silverbell).
  4. Click Create.

3. Mortgage Loan Accounts (Standard Liabilities Group)

Mortgages are formal financial debts, not liquid cash. In Manager.io's architecture, custom Control Accounts are reserved for sub-ledger modules. Mortgages must be managed inside the standard Chart of Accounts using a sub-group.

Step 1: Create the Mortgage Loans Sub-Group

  1. Go to Settings > Chart of Accounts.
  2. Click New Group.
  3. Name it Mortgage Loans and set the Group to Liabilities. Click Create.

Step 2: Create Individual Property Loans

  1. While still in Chart of Accounts, click New Account.
  2. Build the separate property loan records:
    • e.g., Mortgage Loan - Sawtooth and Mortgage Loan - Silverbell):
    • Group: Select the new Mortgage Loans sub-group.
    • Cash Flow Statement: Select Financing activities.
  3. Click Create.

Starting Balances

Manager.io does not use a master global system initialization date. Dates are explicitly tracked inside cash sub-ledgers, while general ledger accounts assume timeless cumulative opening values relative to the first transactional data entry point.

Go to Settings > Starting Balances to input the initial balances:

  • Escrow Balances: Click on Bank and Cash Accounts.
    • Select the escrow account, click New Starting Balance, and
    • Enter the Starting balance and Cleared balance amount
  • Mortgage Loan Balances: Click on Balance Sheet Accounts.
    • Click New Starting Balance,
    • Select the mortgage loan account,
    • Change the type to Credit, and
    • Enter the remaining principal amount owed.

Initialize Loan Cost and Loan Cost Amortization

Transferring from QuickBooks

To initialize your legacy refinancing loan costs from QuickBooks without losing your place in the 30-year amortization schedule, you need to create two distinct parts in Manager.io: an asset account to hold the remaining paper value, and an expense account to handle the annual write-offs.

Because these represent a financial arrangement rather than a physical building, you set them up directly inside your Chart of Accounts as standard ledger lines.

In double-entry accounting, Loan Costs are classified as Assets because they represent a pre-paid legal right that provides your business with a measurable financial benefit over a long period of time.

Step 1: Create the Accounts in your Chart of Accounts

  1. Go to Settings > Chart of Accounts.
  2. Click the New Account button at the top of the Balance Sheet side to create the Sawtooth Asset Row:
    • Name: Unamortized Loan Costs - Sawtooth
    • Group: select Assets.
    • Cash Flow Statement: select Investing activities.
  3. Click Create.
  4. Click New Account again to create the Silverbell Asset Row:
    • Name: Unamortized Loan Costs - Silverbell
    • Group: select Assets.
    • Cash Flow Statement: select Investing activities.
  5. Click Create.
  6. Click New Account on the Profit & Loss Statement side to create your shared expense row:
    • Name: Loan Cost Amortization Expense
    • Group: select your Property Financing Expenses group folder. (See below where we create this as part of Expenses.)
    • Cash Flow Statement: select Operating activities.
  7. Click Create.

Step 2: Input the Clean Net Balances (Starting Balances)

Because these are historical assets, you must hook up their true remaining, unwritten-off net value on Day 1.

Based on your extracted QuickBooks records, those clean net figures are ＄1,861.11 for Sawtooth and ＄1,488.82 for Silverbell.

  1. Go to Settings > Starting Balances.
  2. Click on the row named Balance Sheet Accounts.
  3. Click the New Starting Balance button.
  4. Configure the form for Sawtooth:
    • Account: select Unamortized Loan Costs - Sawtooth.
    • Type: select Debit.
    • Amount: type 1861.11.
  5. Click Create.
  6. Click New Starting Balance again and configure it for Silverbell:
    • Account: select Unamortized Loan Costs - Silverbell.

Putting Into Manager.io

Because your unamortized financing costs live as standard general ledger asset rows, you write down their values using a standard Journal Entry instead.

Step 1: Initialize the Account First

  1. Navigate to Settings > Chart of Accounts.
  2. Click the New Account button on the Profit and Loss Statement side.
  3. Configure the parameters exactly like this:
    • Name: Loan Cost Amortization Expense
    • Group: select your Property Financing Expenses folder (or the main Expenses group).
    • Cash Flow Statement: select Operating activities.
  4. Click Create.

Step 2: Create the Journal Entries

  1. Go to the Journal Entries tab on your left sidebar menu.
  2. Click the New Journal Entry button.
  3. Date: set this to your year-end close (2026-12-31).
  4. Narration: type Annual Refinance Loan Cost Amortization Allocation - 2026.
  5. Check 'on' the option "Column - Description" below the lines.
  6. Configure your 4-line grid rows precisely:
    Line Account Description Debit Credit Division
    1 Loan Cost Amortization Expense Claims the Sawtooth tax deduction 99.22   Sawtooth
    2 Loan Cost Amortization Expense Claims the Silverbell tax deduction 80.26   Silverbell
    3 Unamortized Loan Costs - Sawtooth Ticks your Sawtooth asset balance down   99.22 Sawtooth
    4 Unamortized Loan Costs - Silverbell Ticks your Silverbell asset balance down   80.26 Silverbell
  7. Click Create at the bottom of the page.

Why This Is Flawless and Subtracts Properly

By hitting your Unamortized Loan Costs asset accounts with a Credit on Lines 3 and 4, you directly force their balance sheet totals to drop by exactly ＄99.22 and ＄80.26.

Meanwhile, your property divisions will correctly route the Debits on Lines 1 and 2 to your Profit & Loss statement.

Your final side-by-side tax reports remain 100% correct, your assets update flawlessly, and you have circumvented the software's structural drop-down limitations!

Create Expense Accounts

You need to create your expense accounts. Creating your core list of property expense types all in one go is the best approach.

To prevent your dashboard from becoming cluttered with extraneous, granular accounts, the best accounting architecture for a real estate portfolio is to use a concise list of broad system-level accounts and rely on Manager.io's "Divisions" column to do the granular separation for you.

You do not need to create separate accounts for each property (e.g., you do not need Sawtooth Interest and Silverbell Interest). Instead, you create one global Interest Paid account, and when you assign it to the Sawtooth or Silverbell division on your transaction grid, Manager.io automatically handles the separation on your financial reports [5.1].

1. The Recommended Broad Expense Structure

Here is a highly efficient, IRS-aligned chart of accounts structure tailored to your rental properties. It organizes your expenses under distinct Groups so they collapse cleanly on your main Balance Sheet and Profit & Loss statement:

Group Header: Property Operating Expenses

  • Advertising & Marketing (Leasing signs, online platform listings)
  • HOA Fees (Monthly or quarterly homeowners association dues)
  • Insurance Expense (Landlord property and liability policies)
  • Management Fees (The 10% cut taken by your property management company)
  • Professional & Legal Fees (CPA tax preparation, attorney eviction costs, software subscriptions)
  • Repairs & Maintenance (Plumbing fixes, handymen, turnovers, landscaping, cleaning)
  • Taxes & Licenses (Local business licenses, county property taxes)
  • Utilities (Water, trash, or electric paid by you during vacancies)

Group Header: Property Financing Expenses

  • Interest Paid (The monthly mortgage interest expense portion)
  • Loan Cost Amortization Expense (Your annual flat financing fee write-off - already created.)

2. Step-by-Step: How to Create the Groups and Accounts

To implement this clean structure in the current version of Manager.io, execute the following sequences:

Step A: Create the Collapsible Groups First

  1. Go to Settings > Chart of Accounts.
  2. Click the New Group button at the top right.
  3. Configure the form:
    • Name: Property Operating Expenses
    • Group: select Expenses.
  4. Click Create. (Repeat this exact sequence to create a second group named Property Financing Expenses nested under Expenses).

Step B: Bulk-Create Your Expense Accounts

Now, populate those groups with your clean list of broad accounts:

  1. While still in Chart of Accounts, click the New Account button.
  2. Configure your first target account (e.g., Mortgage Interest):
    • Name: Interest Paid
    • Cash Flow Statements: Operating activities
    • Group: select your newly created Property Financing Expenses group dropdown.
  3. Click Create.
  4. Repeat this quick loop for your remaining broad lines (e.g., Name: HOA Fees, Group: Property Operating Expenses).
    Note: The Accounts won't appear until they contain an active item.

Why This Keep Your Dashboard Elegant

By routing your accounts this way, your main Summary screen stays perfectly compact. Instead of displaying a long, overwhelming list of 50 different rows, it will show just two clean, expandable group headers.

When you run your Side-by-Side Property Profit & Loss Report, the columns will instantly expand horizontally, revealing exactly how much Interest Paid or HOA Fees were spent on Sawtooth versus Silverbell without adding clutter to your workspace!

Setting Up Suppliers (Vendors)

To get your ledger completely ready to handle your imported QBO bank feed line items, you need to register these entities inside Manager.io's Suppliers database.

In double-entry accounting, any entity you pay money to - whether it is an insurance broker, a city tax assessor, a utility company, or a local plumber - is classified as a Supplier (or Vendor). You do not need to create different profiles for different types of payments; they all live together inside a single directory.

Supplier List

Here is exactly how to structure and bulk-create your property network, formatted directly for your notes:

1. Your Required Supplier Registry List

Before you start mapping your bank transactions, create a clean profile row for each of these core operational categories:

  • HOA Payments: Create a supplier profile for the specific association company (e.g., Classic Homeowners Association).
  • Utility Payments: Create individual rows for your regional utility providers (e.g., City Water & Power, Socal Gas Company).
  • Professional Payments: Register your accounting firm or eviction attorneys (e.g., Acme Tax Advisors LLC).
  • Taxation Payments: Register the county government office that handles your real estate assessments (e.g., County Tax Collector Office).
  • Repairs & Purchases: Register your primary maintenance contractors, handymen, or home improvement retail stores (e.g., Reliable Plumbing Services, The Home Depot).
  • Insurance Agents: Register your commercial property insurance carrier or local broker agent (e.g., State Farm Property Insurance).

2. Step-by-Step: How to Set Them Up in Manager.io

Execute this quick creation loop to build your list in one go:

  1. Click on the Suppliers tab located on your primary left sidebar menu.
  2. Click the New Supplier button at the top right of the screen.
  3. Configure the form fields using these strict real estate rules:
    • Name: type the exact legal or trade name of the entity (e.g., County Tax Collector Office).
    • Division: Leave this field BLANK.
  4. Click Create at the bottom of the form.
  5. Repeat this loop for each entity on your list.

💡 Why You Must Leave the "Division" Field Blank

This is a vital rule for your portfolio setup. If you assign a Supplier strictly to the Sawtooth division on their master profile card, the software will completely lock them out of your Silverbell transaction options.

By leaving the Division field blank on the master supplier setup card, you keep them universally available. When you eventually pay a universal store like The Home Depot, you can easily select their name and apply the specific division (Sawtooth or Silverbell) directly onto the individual transaction line items inside your bank feed instead!

Mortgage Servicer as Supplier

In double-entry accounting, your mortgage servicer (e.g., PennyMac Mortgage or Rocket Mortgage) is the vendor to whom you are sending cash disbursements. Registering them as a supplier completes the audit trail, allowing you to link every payment to a verified corporate entity.

Here is exactly how the mortgage servicer features in Manager.io and how they link to your imported QBO bank feed:

1. How the Servicer Features in Your Ledger

The mortgage servicer does not get their own asset or liability account. Instead, they act as the universal "Payee" on your transaction forms, while the money itself is split and routed to your behind-the-scenes tracking accounts.

When you clone your mortgage payment or edit a QBO line, you select the servicer as the Supplier, and then use the line-item grid to break apart the cash:

  • The Principal portion goes to your Liability account (Mortgage Loan - Sawtooth) to shrink your debt.
  • The Interest portion goes to your Expense account (Interest Paid) to claim your tax write-off.
  • The Escrow portion goes to your Asset account (Mortgage Escrow Account - Sawtooth) to track your tax/insurance savings bucket.

2. Step-by-Step: Setting Up Your Servicers

  1. Click on the Suppliers tab on the left sidebar menu.
  2. Click the New Supplier button at the top right.
  3. Configure the card precisely:
    • Name: type the exact name of your lender (e.g., PennyMac Mortgage).
    • Division: leave this field completely BLANK.
  4. Click Create.

Note: Just like your other suppliers, leaving the Division blank ensures that if the same bank holds the loans for both Sawtooth and Silverbell, you can use this single Supplier profile for both properties!

3. Allocating the Imported QBO Mortgage Line

When your mortgage auto-draft appears in your Uncategorized payments list after a QBO import, you will click Edit and map it using Method 2 (The "Clone" Workflow) or by manual entry on the split grid:

  • Supplier Dropdown: Select your newly created Mortgage Servicer Supplier.
  • Line 1: Mortgage Loan - Sawtooth | Division: Sawtooth | Principal Amount
  • Line 2: Interest Paid | Division: Sawtooth | Interest Amount
  • Line 3: Mortgage Escrow Account - Sawtooth | Division: Sawtooth | Escrow Amount

By assigning the lender as the master supplier on the form, Manager.io keeps your transaction history flawlessly organized under your Suppliers registry, making it easy to see exactly how much total cash you have sent to that financial institution over the lifespan of your business.

Rental Income Account

To receive rents from your management company using your Purchase Invoice workflow, you need to initialize a global income account in your directory.

Just like your expense accounts, you only need to create one universal income account for your entire business. You do not need to create separate accounts for each house. When you type your rental figures into the purchase invoice rows and tag the Sawtooth or Silverbell division columns, Manager.io will automatically separate the revenues onto your side-by-side property performance reports!

Here is exactly how to set up your income row with the correct settings:

Create the Income Account

  1. Navigate to Settings > Chart of Accounts.
  2. Click the New Group button at the top of the Profit & Loss Statement side of the summary page.
    • Name: Income
    • Type: Income Group
  3. Click the New Account button at the top of the Profit & Loss Statement side of the summary page.
  4. Configure the parameters exactly like this:
    • Name: Rent Income (or Rental Revenue)
    • Group: select Income from your dropdown list.
    • Cash Flow Statement: select Operating activities.
  5. Click Create.

Property Management Statements (Net Deposits & Contributions)

Property managers deposit net cash totals after collecting gross rent, taking fees, and covering repairs (or they bill you if expenses exceed income). You cannot match these using simple bank rules because the imported QBO text only reflects a single aggregated cash movement. Logging statements via a Purchase Invoice recognizes income/expenses on time and handles both net payouts and owner deficits effortlessly.

Set Up Management as a Supplier

  1. Click on the Suppliers tab on the left sidebar menu.
  2. Click the New Supplier button.
  3. Enter the Property Management Company's name (e.g., Premier Property Management).
  4. Leave the Division field completely *BLANK* (this keeps the vendor universally available across your entire portfolio).
  5. Click Create.

Method A: The Purchase Invoice Workflow (Recommended for Accrual & Shortfalls)

  1. Monthly Processing: The moment the digital property management PDF statement arrives (typically a week before the cash moves), go to the Purchase Invoices tab.
  2. Click New Purchase Invoice and select your Management Company Supplier.
  3. Build out your statement properties line-by-line using *negative numbers for rent revenues* and *positive numbers for expenses*:
    • Line 1: Account: Rent Income | Division: Sawtooth | Amount: *-2500.00*
    • Line 2: Account: Management Fees | Division: Sawtooth | Amount: *250.00*
    • Line 3: Account: Repairs & Maintenance | Division: Sawtooth | Amount: *180.00*
  4. Review the Invoice Total at the bottom:
    • If the Total is Negative (e.g., -＄2,070.00): They owe you money. It registers a pending payout asset under Accounts Payable.
    • If the Total is Positive (e.g., +＄150.00): You owe them an "Owner Contribution" due to high repair costs. It registers a clean liability bill under Accounts Payable.

Allocate Statement to Receipt/Payment

  1. QBO Import & Matching:
    • Ingest your monthly QBO file.
    • Go to the Bank and Cash Accounts tab
    • Click the blue numerical link inside the Uncategorized receipts (for payouts received)
    • Click the blue numerical link inside the Uncategorized payments (for payouts received)
  2. Click Edit on the matching cash line item:
    • Change the single-line account mapping to Accounts Payable
    • Select your Management Company, choose the matching Purchase Invoice
    • Click Update. (This instantly drops your Accounts Payable line to exactly ＄0.00).

Why the Negative Number works:

Because a Purchase Invoice is fundamentally designed to log bills you owe, entering a positive number increases your liabilities. Typing a negative sign in front of your rental income line reverses the math—it forces the system to recognize that money is being earned by you, which perfectly nets against the positive expense rows, establishes your gross revenue trails, and calculates your precise net bank wire down to the penny.

Method B: The Raw Bank Feed Split Strategy (Cash Basis)

  1. Import your monthly QBO bank statement file.
  2. Navigate to the Bank and Cash Accounts dashboard and click the blue hyperlinked number inside your active Uncategorized receipts column.
  3. Locate the net deposit line from your management company and click Edit.
  4. Manually split out the matrix lines to match their PDF statement: *positive numbers for gross rent lines*, and *negative numbers for fees and maintenance rows*.
  5. Assign the corresponding property Division line-by-line and click Update.

Unpredictable Manual Transactions (The "Clone" Workflow)

Manager.io does not support dynamic form templates linked to Payee names, nor do Bank Rules process complex multi-line splits inside a payment form UI.

  • The Workflow: To enter transactions with complex, repeating multi-line structures (like mortgage payments split into principal, escrow, and interest), locate the previous month’s finalized transaction under the Payments or Journal Entries tab.
  • Click on it and select the Clone button at the top of the form screen.
  • This instantly loads a pristine data-entry screen with your suppliers, ledger accounts, and property Divisions pre-copied. Simply update the transaction date and overwrite the rows with the current month's updated numerical amounts.

Multi-Line Escrow & Tax Transactions (The Batch Import Strategy)

Manager.io's Bank Rules engine will drop multi-line allocations into Suspense if the split values do not perfectly balance to the exact net figure imported from a QBO/CSV row. To quickly automate balanced transactions (such as a property tax payment funded out of an escrow account that has a net cash impact of \＄0.00), you bypass bank rules completely and define the splits directly inside a transaction statement spreadsheet.

Step 1: Create a Multi-Line CSV Statement File

Construct a .csv spreadsheet where the balanced transactions are defined on separate lines. By maintaining identical Dates and Descriptions, Manager.io automatically merges the rows into a single multi-line transaction upon upload.

Step 2: Import & Batch Update

  1. Go to Bank and Cash Accounts and open the Escrow Clearing (Dummy) account.
  2. Click Import Bank Statement and upload the file. Match the column headers to map the accounts and divisions.
  3. Click the blue number inside the Uncategorized Payments dashboard column.
  4. Select the newly imported transaction lines, verify that the accounting rows balance to \＄0.00, and click the blue Batch Update button in the bottom corner of the screen to commit them to the ledger.

Annual Straight-Line Building Depreciation Worksheet Automation

Manager.io's native Depreciation Calculation Worksheet uses the Declining Balance method (multiplying the rate against the shrinking book value, not the original purchase cost). To trick the worksheet into generating a flat, fixed US straight-line deduction every single year, you must adjust the percentage value annually.

The Recalculation Workflow

  1. Go to the Fixed Assets tab and find the building's current Net Book Value.
  2. Calculate the custom target worksheet percentage using this formula: 
(Required Annual Straight-Line Building USD Deduction / Current Net Book Value) x 100 = Target Percentage Value
  1. Click Edit on the fixed asset profile and swap the old rate for this new target percentage.
  2. Go to Reports > Depreciation Calculation Worksheet and run the current fiscal period.
  3. CRUCIAL DATE RULE: The worksheet will completely hide an asset if its starting baseline date matches the report's "From" date. Always shift the worksheet's From date to one day later than the asset start date (e.g., if asset starts 2026-01-01, set worksheet From date to 2026-01-02).
  4. Click Create Depreciation Entry at the bottom of the worksheet screen. Tag each row line item with its proper property Division (Sawtooth or Silverbell) and save to push the values to the ledger.
  5. Note: Depreciation entries never dynamically update after creation. If you edit an asset's percentage later, you must open the saved Depreciation Entry, click Edit, and manually overwrite the amount box or delete it and re-run the worksheet.

Reporting Layouts & Comparative Columns

Manager.io handles reporting matrices left-to-right from a baseline column. It cannot add up separate filtered columns horizontally to create a trailing total row. To view the properties side-by-side alongside an unallocated check and a grand total, build the columns in this precise sequence:

  1. Go to Reports > Profit and Loss Statement > New Report.
  2. Column 1 (the Main Base Column): Title it Total Portfolio and leave the Division field completely Blank / Unselected. (This forces the system to pull the grand total company baseline first).
  3. Column 2 (Comparative): Click Add comparative column. Title it Sawtooth and select the Sawtooth division.
  4. Column 3 (Comparative): Click Add comparative column. Title it Silverbell and select the Silverbell division.
  5. Column 4 (Comparative): Click Add comparative column. Title it General Shared and select the General division.
  6. The Result: the generated table will show the global consolidated figures in column 1, and the properties flowing perfectly next to it. Any remaining difference between Column 1 and the property columns represents any unallocated leakage.

Visual Interface & UI Management

  • Reordering the Chart of Accounts: Accounts are not locked alphabetically. To change the layout hierarchy, navigate to Settings > Chart of Accounts. You cannot drag rows on the main master sheet. Instead, click once directly on the double-ended arrow icon next to any item. This opens an isolated sub-level popup screen where you can drag and drop items vertically into the perfect layout order. Click Update to save.
  • Collapsing Groups vs. Hyperlink Access: Adding sub-groups to the Summary Page > Edit > Groups to Collapse setting hides nested lines to make the dashboard neat, but it turns the text headers gray and disables clicking on them. If you need regular access to the mortgage accounts or asset lines, remove them from the "Groups to Collapse" setting so they remain active, fully interactive blue hyperlinks.
  • Interface Scaling & Fonts: Core system fonts cannot be changed. To globally scale the text and interface sizes, hold Ctrl / Cmd and use the + or - keys to instantly zoom the responsive web wrapper layout up or down.
  • Business Logos on Reports: Manager.io natively restricts corporate logos to customer-facing documents (Invoices, Receipts). Internal compliance sheets are kept plain.
