Initialize New Loan Costs and Amortization (New Property)

Overview

Loan origination costs (points, underwriting, appraisal, and title/lender fees) paid at closing are classified as Assets rather than immediate expenses. They are capitalized on the Balance Sheet at full closing cost and amortized evenly over the life of the loan (e.g., 360 months for a 30-year mortgage).

To set up a new loan in Manager.io:

  • Create an Asset sub-group and Asset account to hold the initial closing loan costs.
  • Create an Expense account for annual tax write-offs.
  • Record the initial loan costs via your Closing Journal Entry (or Payment) rather than Starting Balances.

Step 1: Set Up the Chart of Accounts

Go to Settings > Chart of Accounts:

  1. Create the Asset Sub-Group (Balance Sheet side):
    • Click New Group.
    • Name: Financing & Borrowing Costs
    • Group: Assets
    • Click Create.
  2. Create the Property Asset Account (Balance Sheet side):
    • Click New Account:
      • Name: Unamortized Loan Costs - [Property Name]
      • Group: Financing & Borrowing Costs
      • Cash Flow Statement: Investing activities
      • Click Create.
  3. Create the Amortization Expense Account (Profit & Loss side):
    • Click New Account:
      • Name: Loan Cost Amortization Expense
      • Group: Property Financing Expenses (or main Expenses group)
      • Cash Flow Statement: Operating activities
      • Click Create.

Step 2: Record Initial Loan Costs at Closing

Unlike legacy migrations, new loan costs are recorded on your closing date directly from your Closing Disclosure (CD) / HUD-1 statement—not via Starting Balances.

  • If rolled into the new mortgage or paid at escrow closing: Include the total loan origination fees as a Debit line inside your property purchase Journal Entry (dated on the closing date):
    Line Account Description Debit Credit Division
    1 Unamortized Loan Costs - [Property Name] Loan origination fees from Closing [Total $]   [Property Name]
  • If paid directly out of your business bank account: Record a Payment from your Checking account categorized to Unamortized Loan Costs - [Property Name].

Step 3: Calculate Annual Amortization

Determine your annual write-off using straight-line amortization over the loan term:

  • Monthly Amortization: Total Loan Costs / Total Loan Months (e.g., 360 for a 30-year loan).
  • First Year (Prorated): Monthly Amortization × Number of Months Loan Was Active in Year 1 (including the closing month).
  • Full Subsequent Years: Monthly Amortization × 12.

Step 4: Record Annual Year-End Amortization

At year-end, write down the asset balance using a 2-line Journal Entry:

  1. Go to Journal Entries > New Journal Entry.
  2. Date: YYYY-12-31
  3. Narration: Annual Loan Cost Amortization - [Property Name] - YYYY
  4. Check Column - Description below the line items and enter:
    Line Account Description Debit Credit Division
    1 Loan Cost Amortization Expense Annual loan cost tax deduction [Annual $]   [Property Name]
    2 Unamortized Loan Costs - [Property Name] Reduces unamortized asset balance   [Annual $] [Property Name]
  5. Click Create.
