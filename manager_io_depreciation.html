Depreciation

Depreciation Entries

In the latest version of Manager.io, you should use the dedicated Depreciation Entries functional tab alongside the Fixed Assets module to track your property write-offs.

Because you manage multiple rental properties, the software allows you to map your depreciation expenses line-by-line directly to your individual property Divisions (Sawtooth, Silverbell, or General).

Step 1: Enable the Modules

If you don't see these tabs on your left menu, turn them on:

  1. Click Customize Control at the bottom of the left sidebar.
  2. Check the boxes for Fixed Assets and Depreciation Entries.
  3. Click Update.

Step 2: Establish the Property Asset Record

Before you can depreciate a building, it must exist as a sub-ledger entity:

  1. Go to the Fixed Assets tab and click New Fixed Asset.
  2. Set the name (e.g., Sawtooth - Building Value).
  3. Crucial Property Rule: Land does not depreciate. Do not include the land purchase price here. Only enter the structural, depreciable value of the building or capital improvements (like a new roof).
  4. Save the asset record.

Step 3: Enter the Depreciation Transaction

You can record your depreciation at the end of the year or at the end of each financial period:

  1. Go to the Depreciation Entries tab.
  2. Click the New Depreciation Entry button.
  3. Configure the form fields:
    • Date: The date you want the expense to hit your books (e.g., 2026-12-31).
    • Fixed Asset: Select your asset profile (e.g., Sawtooth - Building Value).
    • Division: Select the corresponding property Division (Sawtooth). This ensures the deduction hits that property's specific report columns!
    • Amount: Enter the exact calculated depreciation value (e.g., 5000.00).
  4. Click Create.

Enter the Value

In Manager.io, when you go to the Fixed Assets tab and click New Fixed Asset, there is purposefully no field on that form to type in the asset's purchase price / value.

Manager.io separates the creation of an asset profile from the funding of that asset. When you first create the profile, its acquisition cost defaults to a blank dash (-). To actually give that asset a real financial value, you must assign a transaction or a starting balance to it.

Depending on whether you are bringing over a historical value or logging a brand-new transaction, use the appropriate method below to enter its value:

Method 1: If it is a Historical / Existing Property (Opening Balance)

If you already owned the property before your tracking start date, you assign its opening value via the initialization screen:

  1. Go to Settings > Starting Balances > Fixed Assets (or choose Bank and Cash / Balance Sheet Accounts depending on your layout sub-menus).
  2. Click Edit next to your property (e.g., Sawtooth - Building Value).
  3. You will see two hidden financial fields appear:
    • Starting Balance (Acquisition Cost): Type the original cost basis of the building layout (e.g., 250000.00).
    • Starting Balance (Accumulated Depreciation): Type any write-offs you claimed in prior tax years before migrating to Manager.io (e.g., 15000.00).
  4. Click Save. Your asset register will now perfectly display its true net book value.

Method 2: If It Is a Brand-New Property Purchase

If you just bought the house during your active tracking period, you establish its value by logging a real banking transaction:

  1. Go to your Payments tab or Purchase Invoices tab.
  2. Create a new transaction representing the purchase settlement.
  3. In the line-items grid, do not select an expense account. Instead:
    • Account: Select Fixed Assets, at cost.
    • Fixed Asset: A new dropdown will pop open. Select Sawtooth - Building Value.
    • Amount: Type the transaction settlement cost (e.g., 250000.00).
  4. Click Create.

Why Manager.io Uses This Layout Method

By forcing you to back up an asset's cost with either a starting balance entry or a banking payment record, Manager.io prevents artificial numbers from just showing up out of nowhere.

Once either of these steps is completed, go back to your main Fixed Assets tab dashboard. You will see the blank dash has vanished and your real, auditable property capital value is loaded into the ledger.

Now that you know how to lock in the property acquisition costs:

  • Are you setting up historical property values via Starting Balances?
  • Or are you logging a brand-new purchase transaction from this year?

💡 The Automated Approach (Using Worksheets)

If you prefer not to calculate your depreciation figures manually, modern builds of Manager.io can calculate them automatically using a native worksheet tool:

  1. Go to Fixed Assets and click Edit on your property asset record.
  2. Enter your annual depreciation percentage rate inside the Annual depreciation rate text field and save.
  3. Go to the Reports tab and select Depreciation Calculation Worksheet.
  4. Click New Report, enter your date range, and the software will dynamically compute the exact daily-rate write-off for your rental properties and generate the matching depreciation entries instantly.

The Ledger Impact

Once saved, Manager.io automatically updates your financials without requiring manual journal entries. It debits your Fixed assets - depreciation expense account (reducing net profit under your specific property column path on the P&L) and credits Fixed assets, accumulated depreciation (lowering the book value of your property asset on your Balance Sheet).

The Depreciation Calculation Worksheet

The Depreciation Calculation Worksheet natively calculates tax write-offs using the declining balance method. It evaluates the exact asset book value and computes depreciation on a highly accurate daily rate based on the number of days inside your specified tracking period.

Follow these steps to run the worksheet and let Manager.io auto-generate your property write-offs:

Step 1: Assign the Depreciation Rates to the Properties

Before running the report, you must define the annual depreciation rate directly inside your property asset sub-ledger profiles:

  1. Go to the Fixed Assets tab on the left sidebar.
  2. Click Edit next to your first depreciable building record (e.g., Sawtooth - Building Value).
  3. Locate the Depreciation rate text field and enter your annual percentage as a number (e.g., type 3.636 if you are writing off a residential property using the standard US 27.5-year timeline).
  4. Click Update to save. (Repeat this for your Silverbell building asset using its correct rate).

Step 2: Generate the Calculation Worksheet

  1. Go to the Reports tab on your left sidebar menu.
  2. Under the Fixed Assets heading, click on Depreciation Calculation Worksheet.
  3. Click the New Report button.
  4. Configure the Parameters:
    • From: Select the starting date of your current fiscal period (e.g., 2026-01-01).
    • To: Select the close date of the period (e.g., 2026-12-31).
    • Description: Give it a clear name (e.g., 2026 Annual Property Depreciation).
  5. Click Create.

Step 3: Run the Automation Workflow

When the report renders on your screen, it displays a comprehensive audit grid. You will see your property buildings listed as row headers alongside columns detailing their Starting Book Value, the Daily Rate Calculation, and the Calculated Depreciation Amount for that date window.

To officially push these numbers to your books without typing them line-by-line:

  1. Look at the very bottom left corner of the generated worksheet screen.
  2. Click the New Depreciation Entry button.
  3. Manager.io will instantly open a pre-filled, balanced, multi-line transaction. It will automatically populate the exact calculated dollar figures, match them to your buildings, and link them to your proper Chart of Accounts items (Fixed assets - depreciation and Fixed assets, accumulated depreciation).
  4. The Property Division Step: Because this auto-generated page lists all your assets on separate rows, make sure to look at each row and select the matching property Division dropdown (Sawtooth or Silverbell) so the expense hits your property reports correctly.
  5. Click Create at the bottom of the page to finalize the entry.

⚠️ A Critical Structural Rule for New Assets

Because Manager.io applies depreciation from the absolute "From" date specified on your report range, if you buy a new property midway through the year, you must create a separate worksheet for it. Run its unique worksheet with the "From" date set precisely to its day of acquisition through the end of the year to avoid over-calculating its write-off days.

Summary Page Layout

Your asset cost and its accumulated depreciation should always be paired together in a sub-group called Fixed Assets (or Property, Plant & Equipment). This keeps your main Summary page clean while automatically displaying your property’s true Net Book Value.

Here is exactly how to set up the group and collapse it in the latest version of Manager.io:

Step 1: Create the Fixed Assets Group

  1. Go to Settings > Chart of Accounts.
  2. Click the New Group button at the top right.
  3. Configure the form:
    • Name: Fixed Assets (or Real Estate Portfolio Asset Value)
    • Group: Select Assets.
  4. Click Create.

Step 2: Move the Accounts Into the New Group

  1. While still inside the Chart of Accounts screen, look under your Assets section.
  2. Click Edit next to Fixed Assets, at cost.
  3. Change the Group dropdown from Assets to your newly created Fixed Assets sub-group.
  4. Click Update.
  5. Repeat this exact process for Fixed Assets, accumulated depreciation, moving it into the same Fixed Assets sub-group.

Step 3: Collapse the Group on Your Summary Dashboard

To hide these two rows from cluttering your view, tell the Summary tab to condense them:

  1. Click on the main Summary tab on your left sidebar menu.
  2. Click the Edit button in the top right corner.
  3. Scroll down to the Groups to collapse dropdown field.
  4. Select your custom Fixed Assets group.
  5. Click Save or Update at the bottom.

The Clean Result

Your dashboard will now display a single row named Fixed Assets. Manager.io will automatically do the math behind the scenes—subtracting your accumulated depreciation from your historical purchase costs—and display your exact real estate portfolio equity value in one clean, summarized number.

Grayed out

When you add a group to the Groups to collapse setting, Manager.io turns the main group title gray and disables clicking on it. The software does this because it is displaying a frozen, high-level summary total; it cannot open a single ledger view for a collapsed group because that group contains multiple completely different accounts (at cost and accumulated depreciation) mixed together.

If you want to view the transaction details inside those grayed-out asset accounts, use any of these three quick alternative paths:

Path 1: The "Fixed Assets" Tab (Recommended)

Since you activated the sub-ledger module, you don't need the Summary tab to look at your property data:

  1. Click directly on the Fixed Assets tab on your left sidebar menu.
  2. Here, you will see your properties (Sawtooth, Silverbell) listed cleanly.
  3. You can click on the blue numerical figures under the Acquisition cost or Accumulated depreciation columns to view their complete, itemized transaction history.

Path 2: Uncollapse the Group Temporarily

If you prefer the Summary view for auditing your accounts:

  1. Click Edit at the top right of your Summary dashboard tab.
  2. Remove Fixed Assets from the Groups to collapse field and hit Save.
  3. The rows will unroll. They will turn back into clickable blue hyperlinks so you can click them directly, examine your entries, and then re-collapse the group when you are finished.

Path 3: The Chart of Accounts Screen

  1. Go to Settings > Chart of Accounts.
  2. Find the accounts nested inside your sub-group. They remain fully interactive here. You can click on the balance figures to run your deep-dive audits.

Historic Depreciation Integration

Step 1: Initialize Your Asset Cost and Historical Depreciation

Because you have enabled the Fixed Assets sub-ledger tab, you do not use the plain "Balance Sheet Accounts" screen for your buildings.

Navigate directly to the dedicated sub-ledger initialization page:

  1. Go to Settings > Starting Balances.
  2. Click on the row for Fixed Assets.
  3. Click the New Starting Balance button.
  4. Configure the form precisely like this: • Fixed Asset: Drop down the menu and choose your first asset (e.g., Sawtooth - Building Value). • Starting Balance (Acquisition Cost): Type your Building Basis value (original purchase price minus land value). • Starting Balance (Accumulated Depreciation): Type the total cumulative depreciation claimed on that property from your purchase date up until your current tracking year.
  5. Click Create at the bottom of the form. (Repeat this exact step for your Silverbell property building).

Step 2: Establish Your Non-Depreciable Land Basis

Land must never be mixed into your depreciable building asset line, or Manager.io's calculation worksheets will write off too much money. Because land never depreciates, it is built as a standard, standalone asset account:

  1. Go to Settings > Chart of Accounts.
  2. Click New Account and create an asset named Property Land Value (set its group to Assets).
  3. Now, go to Settings > Starting Balances.
  4. Click on Balance Sheet Accounts (this is where standard ledger rows live).
  5. Scroll to the Assets section, find your new Property Land Value row, and enter your Land Basis value in the Debit column.
  6. Click Save.

Step 3: Verify Your Net Book Value Baseline

Once those two steps are saved, your historical foundation is completely established. To verify the math:

  1. Click directly on the Fixed Assets tab on your left sidebar menu.
  2. Look at your dashboard grid. You will see that Manager.io has automatically calculated your starting point:

Acquisition Cost (Building) - Historical Accumulated Depreciation = Current Net Book Value

When you eventually run your first live Depreciation Calculation Worksheet for the current tax year, the system will read this Net Book Value as its baseline and automatically compute your new depreciation correctly going forward.
