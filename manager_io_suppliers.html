Setting Up Suppliers (Vendors)

To get your ledger completely ready to handle your imported QBO bank feed line items, you need to register these entities inside Manager.io's Suppliers database.

In double-entry accounting, any entity you pay money to - whether it is an insurance broker, a city tax assessor, a utility company, or a local plumber - is classified as a Supplier (or Vendor). You do not need to create different profiles for different types of payments; they all live together inside a single directory.

Supplier List

Here is exactly how to structure and bulk-create your property network, formatted directly for your notes:

1. Your Required Supplier Registry List

Before you start mapping your bank transactions, create a clean profile row for each of these core operational categories:

  • HOA Payments: Create a supplier profile for the specific association company (e.g., Classic Homeowners Association).
  • Utility Payments: Create individual rows for your regional utility providers (e.g., City Water & Power, Socal Gas Company).
  • Professional Payments: Register your accounting firm or eviction attorneys (e.g., Acme Tax Advisors LLC).
  • Taxation Payments: Register the county government office that handles your real estate assessments (e.g., County Tax Collector Office).
  • Repairs & Purchases: Register your primary maintenance contractors, handymen, or home improvement retail stores (e.g., Reliable Plumbing Services, The Home Depot).
  • Insurance Agents: Register your commercial property insurance carrier or local broker agent (e.g., State Farm Property Insurance).

2. Step-by-Step: How to Set Them Up in Manager.io

Execute this quick creation loop to build your list in one go:

  1. Click on the Suppliers tab located on your primary left sidebar menu.
  2. Click the New Supplier button at the top right of the screen.
  3. Configure the form fields using these strict real estate rules:
    • Name: Type the exact legal or trade name of the entity (e.g., County Tax Collector Office).
    • Division: Leave this field BLANK.
  4. Click Create at the bottom of the form.
  5. Repeat this loop for each entity on your list.

💡 Why You Must Leave the "Division" Field Blank

This is a vital rule for your portfolio setup. If you assign a Supplier strictly to the Sawtooth division on their master profile card, the software will completely lock them out of your Silverbell transaction options.

By leaving the Division field blank on the master supplier setup card, you keep them universally available. When you eventually pay a universal store like The Home Depot, you can easily select their name and apply the specific division (Sawtooth or Silverbell) directly onto the individual transaction line items inside your bank feed instead!

3. A Quick Update on Your Property Management Setup

In your query, you mentioned you set up your property management company as a "Customer." Because they send you net cash, it feels natural to treat them as a customer. However, because we are using the Purchase Invoice (Bill) workflow to cleanly log their complex statements a week early, you must go to your Suppliers tab and create a profile for them there too. This ensures their name populates correctly inside your Accounts Payable dropdown menu.

You should delete the Customer listing and keep them strictly as a Supplier. You do not want two separate listings for the exact same company.

Having two profiles for one entity creates a split trail where some transactions hit Accounts Receivable and others hit Accounts Payable. This breaks your automated tracking and makes it difficult to see what they truly owe you.

Why Supplier is the Only Option You Need

Even though they send you a net cash payment every month, they are acting as a service vendor who provides complex billing summaries [5.1]. By keeping them solely as a Supplier, you unlock the clean Purchase Invoice (Bill) workflow we built earlier:

  1. The Statement: You log their statement as a Purchase Invoice under their single Supplier profile. Manager.io computes the net total [5.1].
  2. The Result: If they owe you money, a negative balance is held cleanly inside your Accounts Payable ledger under that supplier card.
  3. The Cleared Wire: One week later, when the cash wire hits your QBO bank feed, you simply assign that incoming deposit directly to Accounts Payable > [Management Company Supplier Name].

This single assignment automatically zeroes out the pending bill, keeps your ledger balanced, and eliminates the need for a separate Customer card entirely.

🛠️ How to Fix This in Your Ledger Right Now

  1. Go to the Customers tab on your left sidebar menu.
  2. Click the Edit button next to your property management company's name.
  3. Scroll to the bottom and click Delete (if you have already recorded transactions against it, Manager.io will warn you; if that happens, just leave it alone for now).
  4. Go to the Suppliers tab on your left sidebar menu.
  5. Click New Supplier and type exactly: Premier Property Management (or their actual company name). Leave the division field blank and click Create.

QUESTION

What if there are more expenses than income via the Management Company on a given month - how do I account for 'owner contributions' (where I have to input a payment to cover expenses above and beyond the rental income)?

ANSWER

When property expenses exceed your rental income, your monthly management statement will calculate a positive total balance - meaning you owe the management company money.

To cover this shortfall, you will wire cash from your business checking account to the management company. In accounting terms, this is not an expense or a capital drop; it is a standard cash disbursement to clear a supplier liability.

Because you are using the Purchase Invoice workflow, handling this shortfall and the subsequent "owner contribution" payment is incredibly clean and requires just two sequential steps:

Step 1: Record the Deficit Statement (The Invoice)

When you receive the PDF statement showing you are in the red, go to your Purchase Invoices tab and log the document exactly as you usually do. Type your expenses as positive numbers and your gross rent collections as negative numbers.

For example, if you had an emergency roof repair on Sawtooth that wiped out your collections, your grid rows would look like this:

Row Account Division Description / Memo Amount
Line 1 Rent Income (Income) Sawtooth Gross Rent Collected -2500.00
Line 2 Management Fees (Expense) Sawtooth Monthly Management Fee 250.00
Line 3 Repairs & Maintenance Sawtooth Emergency Roof Repair 3100.00
  • The Result: Because your repair costs were higher than the rent, Manager.io will calculate a positive final total of +＄850.00 at the bottom of the form.
  • What the system does: The moment you click Create, Manager.io records your gross rental income and your exact plumbing expenses, and then parks an active ＄850.00 debt inside your Accounts Payable ledger under that supplier's profile card.

Step 2: Record the Owner Contribution Payment (The QBO Clearing Wire)

A few days later, you transfer ＄850.00 out of your physical business checking account to cover the bill. When that outgoing transaction appears inside your Uncategorized payments bank feed column, you clear it with a single, direct allocation:

  1. Locate the outgoing transfer line item inside your raw bank feed list.
  2. Click the Edit button next to it.
  3. Configure the single-line row fields exactly like this:
    • Account: select Accounts Payable from the primary dropdown.
    • Supplier: select your property management company profile.
    • Invoice: select the specific deficit purchase invoice you logged in Step 1.
  4. Click Update.

Why This Workflow Handles Shortfalls Flawlessly

The exact moment you save that payment allocation, the outgoing cash matches up against the pending debt. Your Accounts Payable balance drops back down to exactly ＄0.00, your business bank register reconciles to the penny, and your monthly property performance charts remain perfectly intact without you ever having to invent any strange "contribution" accounts or create artificial adjustments!
