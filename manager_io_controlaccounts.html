Control Accounts

In the latest version of Manager.io, a Control Account is a special, top-level Balance Sheet account that acts as a summary container for a group of individual sub-accounts (known as subsidiary ledgers).

Instead of cluttering your main Balance Sheet with dozens of individual names or bank accounts, Manager.io uses Control Accounts to combine them into single line items to keep your financial statements clean and professional.

Understanding how they work in your rental property context is straightforward:

1. Built-in vs. Custom Control Accounts

Manager.io uses two types of Control Accounts:

  • Built-in Control Accounts: When you enable tabs like Customers or Suppliers, Manager.io automatically creates a built-in control account called Accounts Receivable or Accounts Payable. You never post transactions directly to "Accounts Receivable"—instead, you post a transaction to Tenant John Smith, and Manager.io automatically updates the total under the Accounts Receivable control account.
  • Custom Control Accounts: The latest versions allow you to create your own custom groups. For example, instead of all your properties' bank and escrow accounts clumping into one generic row, you can go to Settings > Control Accounts and create a specific control account called Property Escrow Accounts.

2. How it Directly Applies to Your Rentals

As a rental owner, you will most commonly use Control Accounts to group your Bank, Cash, and Mortgage Escrow accounts.

By default, Manager.io lumps every single bank account you create under one row on your Balance Sheet called Cash & Cash Equivalents. For a property owner with multiple checking accounts and escrow accounts, that gets confusing quickly.

You can use Control Accounts to split them up cleanly:

What you set up in Settings > Control Accounts Individual Sub-Accounts grouped inside them What shows on your final Balance Sheet
Operating Cash (Control Account) * Property A Checking Operating Cash: ＄15,500
  * Property B Checking  
Escrow Cash (Control Account) * Escrow - Property A Escrow Cash: ＄8,200
  * Escrow - Property B  

Key Rule for Control Accounts

You cannot manually select a Control Account on a daily transaction line (like a payment, receipt, or invoice). If you try to pick Accounts Receivable on a payment, Manager.io won't let you. You must select the specific Customer or the specific Bank Account, and let Manager.io auto-summarize the math upward into the Control Account.

Creating Control Accounts

Let’s set up your first Custom Control Accounts in Manager.io.

By separating your properties into dedicated control accounts, your final Balance Sheet will look incredibly clean—grouping all your individual property loans into one neat summary row and all your escrow buckets into another.

Step 1: Create the "Mortgage Loans" Control Account (Liabilities)

First, we will build a container to hold all your individual property mortgages.

  1. Go to Settings on the left menu.
  2. Click on Control Accounts.
  3. Click the New Control Account button.
  4. Fill out the form fields exactly like this:
    • Name: Mortgage Loans
    • Type: Select Liability from the dropdown menu.
  5. Click Create.

Step 2: Create the "Property Escrow Buckets" Control Account (Assets)

Next, we will build a container to group all the separate escrow funds held by your banks.

  1. While still in the Control Accounts screen, click New Control Account again.
  2. Fill out the form fields exactly like this:
    • Name: Property Escrow Buckets
    • Type: Select Asset from the dropdown menu.
  3. Click Create.

Step 3: Link Your Rental Property Accounts to the New Containers

Now that the containers exist, we need to move your specific property accounts inside them so the summary math works automatically.

Move your Escrow Dummy Accounts:

  1. Go to the Bank and Cash Accounts tab on the left menu.
  2. Click Edit next to your first escrow account (e.g., Escrow Account - 123 Main St).
  3. Look for a field labeled Control Account.
  4. Drop down the menu and select Property Escrow Buckets.
  5. Click Update to save. (Repeat this step for your other property escrow accounts).

Move your Mortgage Loan Accounts:

  1. Go to Settings > Chart of Accounts.
  2. Find your specific loan liability account (e.g., Mortgage Loan - 123 Main St) and click Edit.
  3. Look for the Control Account dropdown field.
  4. Select your newly created Mortgage Loans control account.
  5. Click Update to save. (Repeat this step for your other property loan accounts).

The Final Result on Your Balance Sheet

The next time you open your Balance Sheet or look at your main dashboard, you won't see a messy, long list of individual properties clumping up your asset and liability columns.

Instead, you will see two clean rows:

  • Under Assets, you will see a single line item for Property Escrow Buckets displaying the total combined savings across your whole portfolio.
  • Under Liabilities, you will see a single line item for Mortgage Loans showing the total overall principal balance you owe to your lenders.

Creating Real and Control Escrow Accounts

To create your real, individual escrow accounts (which will sit inside your newly created "Property Escrow Buckets" control account), you create them under the Bank and Cash Accounts tab.

Even though you don't write physical checks out of your real-world escrow accounts, Manager.io requires them to be built as Cash Accounts so that you can import your QBO files or fake zero-sum CSV files directly into them.

Here is exactly how to create Mortgage Escrow Account - Sawtooth and Mortgage Escrow Account - Silverbell step-by-step:

Step 1: Open the Bank and Cash Accounts Tab

  1. Click on the Bank and Cash Accounts tab on your left sidebar menu.
  2. Click the New Bank or Cash Account button.

Step 2: Configure the Sawtooth Escrow Account

Fill out the creation form using these exact settings to attach it to your control account and property division:

  1. Name: Type Mortgage Escrow Account - Sawtooth.
  2. Code: (Optional) You can leave this blank.
  3. Currency: Select your currency (e.g., USD).
  4. Control Account: Drop down this menu and select your custom Property Escrow Buckets control account.
  5. Division: Drop down this menu and select your Sawtooth property division.
  6. Click Create at the bottom of the form.

Step 3: Configure the Silverbell Escrow Account

Repeat the exact same process for your second property:

  1. Click New Bank or Cash Account again.
  2. Name: Type Mortgage Escrow Account - Silverbell.
  3. Control Account: Select Property Escrow Buckets.
  4. Division: Select your Silverbell property division.
  5. Click Create.

How this looks on your screen now:

  • In your daily workflow: When you open your Bank and Cash Accounts tab, you will now see Mortgage Escrow Account - Sawtooth and Mortgage Escrow Account - Silverbell listed as distinct ledger books. You can click into either one to import statement files or view its unique transaction history.
  • On your corporate Balance Sheet: These two accounts will completely disappear from view as individual rows. Instead, their balances will automatically combine and display under the single, clean control account row named Property Escrow Buckets.

Loan Accounts

Mortgage accounts are not Cash Accounts. They are Liability Accounts.

In accounting, Cash Accounts are only used for places where liquid money physically sits (like checking, savings, or escrow buckets). A mortgage is debt you owe, so it must be built within your standard Chart of Accounts as a liability.

You should link them to a Control Account to keep your Balance Sheet clean and professional.

Here is exactly how and where to create your individual mortgage accounts (Mortgage Loan - Sawtooth and Mortgage Loan - Silverbell) in the latest version of Manager.io:

Step 1: Navigate to Your Chart of Accounts

  1. Go to Settings in the bottom left menu.
  2. Click on Chart of Accounts.

Step 2: Create the Sawtooth Mortgage Account

  1. Look at the top right of the screen and click the New Account button.
  2. Fill out the form fields exactly like this:
    • Name: Mortgage Loan - Sawtooth
    • Group: Select Liabilities (or Long-term Liabilities if you have sub-groups set up).
    • Control Account: Drop down this menu and select the Mortgage Loans control account we created earlier.
    • Division: Select your Sawtooth property division.
  3. Click Create at the bottom of the form.

Step 3: Create the Silverbell Mortgage Account

  1. Click New Account again.
  2. Fill out the form fields for your second property:
    • Name: Mortgage Loan - Silverbell
    • Group: Select Liabilities.
    • Control Account: Select Mortgage Loans.
    • Division: Select your Silverbell property division.
  3. Click Create.

Why This Setup is Ideal For You

By mapping both mortgages to your Mortgage Loans Control Account and tagging them with their respective Divisions, you achieve perfect tracking symmetry:

  • On Your Main Balance Sheet:
    • Your individual properties won't clutter the view.
    • You will see a single, clean line under Liabilities named Mortgage Loans showing your total portfolio debt.
  • On a Side-by-Side Property Report:
    • If you run a report filtered by columns, Manager.io will beautifully separate the debt across the page.
    • The Sawtooth column will show its specific loan balance, and the Silverbell column will show its specific loan balance, meeting perfectly under the single row header.
DON'T COLLAPSE TO A CONTROL GROUP - IT'S NOT EXPANDABLE.

Starting Balances

In the latest version of Manager.io, you should use the built-in Starting Balances facility under Settings, rather than creating a manual transaction after the fact.

Manager.io has a strict architectural rule: Balance Sheet accounts (like your mortgage loans) must have their opening values set through the official initialization screen to ensure your historical equity and ledger balances remain perfectly synchronized.

Here is exactly how to set up your mortgage starting balances safely:

Step 1: Enable Starting Balances

  1. Go to Settings in the bottom left menu.
  2. Click on Starting Balances.
  3. If this is your first time accessing it, you will see a field prompting you for a Starting Balance Date.
  4. Set this date carefully: Choose the day before your official tracking begins in Manager.io (e.g., if you want your books to start tracking on January 1st, set your starting balance date to December 31st).

Step 2: Input Your Mortgage Balances

Once the starting date is locked in, Manager.io will present you with a structured list of your entire Chart of Accounts.

  1. Scroll down to your Liabilities section.
  2. Locate your two individual accounts: Mortgage Loan - Sawtooth and Mortgage Loan - Silverbell.
  3. Click in the balance fields next to them and enter the exact principal amounts you owe on that starting date.
  4. Click Save at the bottom of the screen.

⚠️ The Balancing Step (What happens next)

Because every entry in accounting requires an equal and opposite reaction, entering those starting liabilities will temporarily throw your balance sheet out of whack.

Manager.io handles this safely by automatically dumping the total offsetting amount into a special equity category called Starting Balance Equity (or Suspense if items are left entirely unmapped).

To balance your books correctly after typing in what you owe:

  • Scroll up to your Assets section on that exact same Starting Balances page.
  • Enter the estimated Fair Market Value or historical cost of your rental properties under your Fixed Assets / Property accounts.
  • Result: Once your property asset values and cash balances match up against your mortgage debt values, the Starting Balance Equity account will zero out, leaving you with an accurate starting equity position for your real estate business.

Which Starting Balance Category?

To enter your opening figures, navigate directly to Settings > Starting Balances.

You will see a broad master menu containing several localized subsections.

Because Manager.io isolates sub-ledger modules from standard general ledger accounts, you must enter your data into specific sections based on the account type.

Use this clear structural breakdown to place your figures exactly where they belong:

  1. Where to Enter Mortgage Liabilities
  2. The Section: Click on Balance Sheet Accounts.
  3. How to enter it: Scroll down to your Liabilities section.
  4. You will see your individual rows for Mortgage Loan - Sawtooth and Mortgage Loan - Silverbell.
  5. The Data: Enter the remaining principal balance you owe to your lender for each property as a Credit (positive liability balance).
  6. Where to Enter Escrow Accounts
  7. The Section: Click on Bank and Cash Accounts.
  8. How to enter it: Because you created your escrow buckets inside the Bank and Cash Accounts module as real sub-ledgers, they will not appear in the plain "Balance Sheet Accounts" section. Click on the Bank and Cash Accounts row inside the Starting Balances menu.
  9. The Data: Locate Mortgage Escrow Account - Sawtooth and Mortgage Escrow Account - Silverbell. Enter the exact dollar amounts currently sitting in those escrow buckets as a Debit (positive cash asset balance).

Quick Guide for Your Other Rental Property Accounts

If you are setting up the rest of your real estate portfolio balances on this page, use this map:

  • Property Buildings / Land (Fixed Assets): If you are tracking the core value of the real estate itself, choose Balance Sheet Accounts.
  • Look under the Assets section for your property asset codes.
  • Unpaid Rent from Old Tenants: If you are migrating historical records and a tenant still owes you back-rent from before your start date, do not type it into Balance Sheet Accounts. Instead, select Sales Invoices.
  • You will enter the old outstanding invoice there so it links directly to that specific Customer (Tenant) profile.
  • Security Deposits Held: If you hold tenant security deposits in a liability account, go to Balance Sheet Accounts and look under your Liabilities section to record those tenant balances.

There is no Starting Date!

In the latest versions of Manager.io, there is no global "Start Date" field anymore. The developer completely removed the requirement to set a master starting date in your settings.

Instead, Manager.io determines your start date implicitly based on the individual dates you type into your transactions and sub-ledger entries.

Because of this design overhaul, the workflow for entering your Mortgages versus your Escrow Accounts behaves slightly differently on those entry screens:

  1. The Escrow Accounts (Under "Bank and Cash Accounts")

When you go to Settings > Starting Balances > Bank and Cash Accounts and click New Starting Balance, you will notice that this specific form does have a Date field.

  • Type the exact date you want your escrow tracking to officially begin (e.g., 2026-01-01).
  • Input your starting cash balance. Manager.io locks that date specifically for that cash ledger.
  • The Mortgage Accounts (Under "Balance Sheet Accounts")

When you click on Settings > Starting Balances > Balance Sheet Accounts, you will notice a plain list of text fields with no date boxes anywhere on the screen.

This is because standard general ledger accounts in Manager.io are timeless cumulative records. The software assumes that whatever numbers you type into this screen represent the static starting position of your business at the exact moment you begin entering your day-to-day transactions.

How to manage this cleanly:

  1. Simply type your remaining mortgage principal balances into those plain liability fields right now [4.3].
  2. When you eventually transition to your normal routine and enter your very first monthly mortgage statement or import your first QBO file, ensure that transaction's date is your true Day 1 (e.g., your January mortgage statement is dated 2026-01-15).
  3. Manager.io will automatically treat your Balance Sheet entries as the structural foundation existing immediately prior to that first transaction date.

Summary Rule

Don't worry about hunting for a master calendar date box on the Balance Sheet screen. Enter the numbers as they stand today [4.3], set the date explicitly inside your Bank and Cash opening balances, and let your first live transaction's date organically signal to Manager.io when your operational tracking officially goes live.

Activity (Type)

On a Statement of Cash Flows, a mortgage loan account is classified as a Financing Activity.

In Manager.io, when you edit a liability account, you will see a dropdown field explicitly asking for its cash flow classification. Selecting Financing Activities ensures your principal payments report correctly.

How the Mortgage Breakdown Affects Cash Flows

A standard monthly mortgage payment spans multiple classifications on a Cash Flow Statement based on where the money goes:

Payment Component Account Type Cash Flow Classification Why?
Principal Paydown Liability Account Financing Activity It changes the size of your business borrowings.
Interest Payment Expense Account Operating Activity It flows through your P&L and enters into net income.
Escrow Deposit Asset Account Operating Activity It behaves like a prepaid operational expense (for future taxes/insurance).

What About the Other Categories?

  • Investing Activities: This is reserved for buying or selling long-term assets. The cash paid to purchase the building or land itself goes here, but the loan used to fund it goes under Financing.
  • Cash and Cash Equivalent Activities: This is strictly reserved for actual liquid money holdings—such as your core business checking account. It reflects the net change in cash on hand at the end of the reporting period.

Alternative to Control Accounts for Liabilities

In Manager.io, you cannot build a custom Control Account for standard general ledger liabilities.

This explains why you do not see a "Liabilities" section on the Control Accounts screen. In Manager.io's architecture, custom Control Accounts under Settings > Control Accounts can only be created to group specific, pre-built subsidiary ledgers (sub-ledgers) like:

  • Bank and Cash Accounts
  • Customers / Suppliers
  • Fixed Assets / Intangible Assets
  • Capital Accounts / Investments

Because you built your mortgages as standard liability accounts inside your Chart of Accounts (and not as cash accounts or fixed asset sub-ledgers), the software does not recognize them as sub-ledgers that require a dedicated Control Account card.

How to Clean Up Your Liabilities Without a Control Account

To clean up your balance sheet and summarize your different property mortgages under a single row, you use Chart of Accounts Groups instead of a Control Account. This achieves the exact same look on your financial statements.

Follow these steps to clean up your liabilities layout:

Step 1: Create a Liability Group

  1. Go to Settings > Chart of Accounts.
  2. Click the New Group button at the top right.
  3. Configure the group form: • Name: Mortgage Loans • Group: Select Liabilities.
  4. Click Create.

Step 2: Move Your Mortgages into the Group

  1. While still inside your Chart of Accounts, locate your individual accounts (Mortgage Loan - Sawtooth and Mortgage Loan - Silverbell).
  2. Click Edit next to Mortgage Loan - Sawtooth.
  3. Locate the Group dropdown menu and change it from Liabilities to your newly created Mortgage Loans sub-group.
  4. Click Update to save. (Repeat this step for the Silverbell account).

The Structural Difference and Why it Works

Now, your Chart of Accounts layout will be structured neatly: 

▼ Liabilities
  ▼ Mortgage Loans (Your Sub-Group)
    • Mortgage Loan - Sawtooth
    • Mortgage Loan - Silverbell
  • On your main Balance Sheet summary dashboard: Manager.io automatically collapses sub-groups. You will see a single row header named Mortgage Loans showing your total portfolio debt, keeping your statements perfectly clean.
  • On your Property Divisions side-by-side columns: When you break down your report horizontally by property, Manager.io will neatly display the Sawtooth column debt and Silverbell column debt on separate column paths under that single Mortgage Loans row header [5.1].

Collapsing the SubGroups

By default, Manager.io displays a hierarchical, indented tree structure on your main Summary dashboard tab. Even if you place accounts inside a custom sub-group, they will stay expanded and visible unless you explicitly configure the layout to condense them.

To collapse your Mortgage Loans group down to a single row on your dashboard, toggle on the collapse option via the Summary settings:

How to Hide Sub-Accounts on the Summary Layout

  1. Click on the main Summary tab on the left sidebar menu.
  2. Look at the top right of your screen and click the Edit button.
  3. Scroll down the settings form and check the box or look for the field labeled Groups to collapse.
  4. Click inside the Groups to collapse dropdown field and select your custom Mortgage Loans group.
  5. Click Save or Update at the bottom of the screen.

Once saved, the nested lines (Mortgage Loan - Sawtooth and Mortgage Loan - Silverbell) will be completely hidden from your dashboard view. They will behave exactly like a single account row named Mortgage Loans, showing your total aggregated portfolio liability. If you ever need to view the property-by-property principal breakdown, you can simply click directly on that total number to drill down into the sub-ledger.

Reconciliation

In the latest version of Manager.io, the "Pending Transactions" configuration option has been moved out of the bank account settings completely.

Instead, the capability to track pending vs. cleared transactions is permanently built into the platform. You do not need to activate any checkbox.

To utilize pending tracking and configure your dashboard columns to view these statuses, use the following layout setup:

Step 1: Reveal the Status Columns on Your Dashboard

  1. Go to the Bank and Cash Accounts tab on the left sidebar menu.
  2. Scroll to the bottom right corner and click Edit Columns.
  3. Check the boxes for Cleared balance, Pending deposits, and Pending withdrawals. Click Save.

Your dashboard will now display these individual balance columns side-by-side. Any transaction causing a discrepancy between your Cleared Balance and Actual Balance represents your outstanding item exception list.

Step 2: How to Set a Transaction to "Pending"

Because the global setting is always active, the status is managed directly inside individual transaction records:

  1. Open any Payment or Receipt transaction entry screen.
  2. Look at the Cleared field at the bottom of the form. By default, it says On the same date (meaning it is instantly cleared).
  3. Click the dropdown menu and change it to On a later date.
  4. The Rule: If you leave the clearing date box completely blank, Manager.io will mark the transaction as Pending. If you pick a specific future date, it remains pending until that calendar date arrives.

When you process your monthly property management reconciliations, any imported QBO or CSV transactions automatically register as Cleared on arrival. You only need to change manual inputs (such as a handwritten maintenance check) to On a later date to hold it in a pending state until it officially posts to your bank statement.
