DBA Setup

Here is a streamlined, step-by-step checklist to launch a combined VFX tutorial and app development side business in Ontario, CA as a Sole Proprietor - keeping startup costs minimal while making expenses 100% audit-ready.

Phase 1: Zero-Cost Federal & Financial Setup (Day 1)

  1. Get a Free Federal EIN (Employer Identification Number):
    • Apply directly on the IRS EIN Assistant (takes 5 minutes, completely free).
    • Why: Even as a Sole Proprietor with no employees, an EIN lets you fill out client W-9 forms, register app developer accounts (Apple/Google/Gumroad), and open bank accounts without handing out your Social Security Number.
  2. Open a Dedicated Checking Account:
    • Open a separate free checking account (either a basic business checking account using a EIN or a dedicated personal checking account used exclusively for the business).
    • Why: Never mix personal groceries/bills with business software, gear, or app store payouts. Clean separation is the #1 thing the IRS looks for to prove a legitimate business.
  3. Set Up Simple Bookkeeping & Receipt Capture:
    • Use a free tool like Wave, a simple spreadsheet, or a dedicated folder in Google Drive.
    • Save PDF receipts for every software license, server fee, microphone, or hardware purchase, and jot down a 1-sentence note on how it supports the tutorials or app development.

Phase 2: Local Ontario & California Compliance (Within 30–40 Days)

  1. Decide on a Business Name:
    • Option A (Free): Operate under your legal name (e.g., "Alex Smith" or "Alex Smith VFX"). Skip the county DBA completely.
    • Option B (Brand Name): If using a studio/brand name (e.g., "RenderCraft Tools"), file a Fictitious Business Name (DBA) with San Bernardino County within 40 days of the first transaction and run the 4-week newspaper notice.
  2. Apply for a City of Ontario Business License:
    • Because you are working from home in Ontario, register for a Home Occupation Business License through the City of Ontario Business License Portal.
    • Cost: Typically around ＄50–＄100/year for small home-based businesses (plus a mandatory ＄4 state CASp fee).

Phase 3: Launch & Tax-Tracking Habits (Ongoing)

  1. Document a "Profit Motive" From Day 1:
    • Launch a basic landing page, GitHub repo, Gumroad store, or YouTube channel right away - even before the first paid product is finished. Having a public web presence proves you are actively building a commercial venture rather than buying gear for a hobby.
  2. Track Home Office Square Footage & Business Use:
    • Measure the exact square footage of any dedicated workspace (and total home square footage) to claim the Home Office Deduction.
    • Log the realistic business-use percentage (e.g., 40% business / 60% personal) for any shared equipment like a primary PC, internet bill, or phone.
  3. Set Aside 25%–30% of Net Profits for Taxes:
    • Once the tutorials or apps start generating profit (income minus expenses), transfer ~25%–30% of the net profit into a separate high-yield savings bucket to cover federal/California income tax and the 15.3% self-employment tax.
