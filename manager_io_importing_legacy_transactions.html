Manager.io - Importing Legacy Transactions via CSV

You can ingest a CSV of historical inputs to populate your Capital accounts instead of entering a single, flat starting balance.

In double-entry accounting, importing transactions rather than using a flat starting balance is a highly recommended practice. It leaves a bulletproof, historical audit trail directly inside your new ledger. It proves exactly who contributed how much cash and when over the lifetime of your partnership.

To achieve this cleanly without cluttering your main active operational bank feeds, you will use Manager.io's Receipts Import Pipeline routed directly into a specialized structural staging ledger.

Step 1: Format Your Historical Capital Input CSV File

Open Vim, Excel, or a text editor and prepare your historical contribution data as a standard CSV file. Ensure it contains these exact, simple column rows: 

Date,Description,Partner Account,Amount
2014-03-15,Partner Capital Contribution - Partner A,Partner A,15000.00
2014-04-10,Partner Capital Contribution - Partner B,Partner B,15000.00
2016-08-22,Emergency Repair Assessment Input - Partner A,Partner A,4500.00

Step 2: Initialize the Capital Sub-Accounts

Before importing, make sure Manager.io has the unique identity profiles for both partners ready to receive the data:

  1. Go to Settings > Capital Accounts.
  2. Click New Capital Account for Partner A.
  3. Click Create.
  4. Click New Capital Account again for Partner B.
  5. Click Create.

Step 3: Ingest the CSV Using a Staging Ledger

Because these historical capital infusions have already cleared your real-world banks years ago, do not import this CSV into your live active Checking account feed. Doing so will completely break your current reconciliation. Instead, you create a temporary staging "Clearing bank account" to act as your import vacuum:

  1. Go to Bank and Cash Accounts > New Bank Account.
  2. Name it Historical Capital Staging Account.
  3. Click Create.
  4. Look at the bottom of the Bank Accounts pane, and click Import Bank Statement.
  5. Choose the new account from the dropdown (Historical Capital Staging Account)
  6. Choose your prepared CSV file, map the columns (Date, Description, Amount)
  7. Click Import.

Step 4: Rectify the Imported Inputs to the Capital Ledger

Now, all your historical partner cash inputs are sitting inside your staging queue as Uncategorized receipts. You will map them permanently using a single batch screen:

  1. Click on the blue Uncategorized receipts number link next to your Historical Capital Staging Account.
    • (Note: the Uncategorized columns must be activated.)
  2. For each historical row, set the allocation parameters:
    • Account: select Capital Accounts.
    • Sub-account: select the specific profile (Partner A - Capital or Partner B - Capital).
    • Division: Leave blank (unless an input was explicitly earmarked for a single house structural down payment).
  3. Scroll to the bottom and click Batch Update

Why This Workflow Is Superior to a Flat Starting Balance

  • Permanent Database History: If you drill down into either partner's capital account on the Summary screen, Manager.io will reveal an interactive ledger dating back years. You can search, filter, and sort every single input dollar.
  • Balanced Accounting Migration: Once all receipts are allocated, your Capital Accounts equity rows on the right-hand side of your P&L will perfectly reflect your true historical capital baselines. Your Historical Capital Staging Account asset balance will perfectly match the cash total injected. You can then cleanly clear out that staging balance against your properties' historical book assets using a single closing Journal Entry!

Historical Capital Contributions Migration (CSV Import Protocol)

Instead of entering a single flat Starting Balance, importing historical partner contributions via CSV builds a permanent, chronological audit trail inside Manager.io showing every date, memo, and dollar amount across the years.

1. Prepare the Raw CSV File

Manager.io's Bank Rule engine relies on exact column headers to parse text and route transactions automatically. Format your source CSV with these three mandatory headers:

  • Date (e.g., 2014-03-15)
  • Description (Must contain a distinct keyword phrase so Bank Rules can catch it)
  • Amount (Positive numbers for contributions/inputs; negative numbers for owner draws)

Single-Owner vs. Two-Partner CSV Labeling

  • Single Owner: Use a uniform label in the Description column (e.g., Capital Contribution).
  • Two Partners (Recommended): Embed the specific partner's name into the Description string so Manager.io can route each partner's history into their own Equity ledger automatically: 
    Date,Description,Amount
2014-03-15,Capital Contribution - Partner A,15000.00
2014-04-10,Capital Contribution - Partner B,15000.00
2016-08-22,Capital Contribution - Partner A,-2500.00

2. Initialize the Equity & Staging Structure

Before importing the CSV, set up isolated containers so historical transactions never mix with your active Checking account.

Step A: Set Up the Partner Equity Control Accounts (Shows Partners on Summary)

By default, Manager.io lumps all Capital Accounts into one single line on the Summary screen. To display each partner individually on the Equity Summary:

  1. Go to Settings > Control Accounts > New Control Account.
  2. Create a Control Account for each partner (e.g., Partner A - Investments and Partner B - Investments) assigned to the *Equity* group.
  3. (Optional) Create a visual *Group* under Settings > Chart of Accounts (Equity side) named Partner Capital and nest both Control Accounts inside it.
  4. Go to the Capital Accounts tab on the left sidebar and click New Capital Account for each partner, linking each profile to its matching custom Control Account.

Step B: Create an Isolated Staging Bank Account

  1. Go to Settings > Control Accounts > New Control Account and create Historical Staging Control (Group: *Assets*).
  2. Go to Bank and Cash Accounts > New Bank Account and create Historical Capital Staging Account, explicitly assigning it to Historical Staging Control (never leave it blank, or it may default to your live Checking account).

3. Configure the Receipt & Payment Bank Rules

Create Bank Rules before importing so Manager.io catches and assigns every row upon arrival. (Create separate rules for Partner A and Partner B if tracking two partners).

  1. Go to Settings > Bank Rules > New Receipt Rule (catches positive contributions):
    • Bank account: Historical Capital Staging Account
    • Description contains: Capital Contribution - Partner A (or Capital Contribution for single owner)
    • Account: Capital accounts -> Select Partner A - Investments
    • Although in my case it was:
      • Account: Owner - Investements Capital Account: Select Owner - Investments
    • Click Create.
  2. Go to Settings > Bank Rules > New Payment Rule (catches negative draws/withdrawals):
    • Apply the exact same Bank Account, Description keyword, and Capital Account routing.
    • Click Create.

4. Ingest & Commit the CSV

  1. Go to Bank and Cash Accounts, locate Historical Capital Staging Account, and click Import Bank Statement.
  2. Select your prepared CSV file, verify the Date, Description, and Amount column mappings, and click Import.
  3. Click the blue numerical links inside the Uncategorized receipts (and Uncategorized payments) columns for the staging account.
  4. Verify that the rows show Suspense crossed out and replaced by your partner Capital Account(s).
  5. Select all staged rows and click Batch Update to commit them permanently into the Equity ledger.

5. Zero Out the Staging Account (Final Balance Sheet Cleanup)

Because those historical contributions cleared your real-world bank accounts years ago, your Historical Capital Staging Account will now display a cumulative cash balance on your Assets card equal to the total historical inputs.

To clear that temporary asset balance without erasing your newly imported Capital history:

  1. Note the exact final balance sitting in Historical Capital Staging Account.
  2. Record a single balancing Journal Entry (or Payment from the staging account) dated on your migration cutover date (e.g., 2026-01-01) that credits Historical Capital Staging Account down to 0.00 and debits your historical migration offset/asset baseline.
  3. Once the staging account balance reaches 0.00, you can mark it Inactive (or leave it hidden) while your Partner Investments Equity accounts preserve the complete multi-year transaction database.

Zero the Legacy Setup Accounts

To zero out the values sitting in the Historical Staging Control asset account without touching your Owner - Investments history, record a single Journal Entry that routes the offset into your historical retained earnings / migration equity account (Cumulative Structural Tax Deductions).

Why This Works

In double-entry accounting, your Historical Staging Control asset represents the cash you put into the business over the years. In reality, that cash was already spent on your buildings, land, and historical operations—which are currently offset inside your Cumulative Structural Tax Deductions (Retained Earnings) account.

By crediting the staging bank account and debiting that migration equity account, you erase the phantom asset while leaving every single row inside Owner - Investments completely intact.

Step-by-Step: Create the Clearing Journal Entry

  1. Click on the Journal Entries tab on your left sidebar menu.
  2. Click the New Journal Entry button at the top right.
  3. Configure the header fields:
    • Date: Set this to your migration cutover date (e.g., 2026-01-01).
    • Narration: Zero out historical capital staging bank account against migration retained earnings
  4. Build this exact 2-line grid:

Here we are assuming a value of ＄194680.55:

Line Account Sub-account / Bank Account Debit Credit Description / Memo
1 Cumulative Structural Tax Deductions Leave Blank 194680.55   Absorbs historical cash already deployed into properties/operations
2 Historical Staging Control Historical Capital Staging Account   194680.55 Zeroes out the temporary staging bank account
  1. Click Create at the bottom of the screen.

Verify Your Summary Screen

When you return to your main Summary dashboard:

  • Historical Staging Control drops to 0.00 and disappears from your active Assets card.
  • Owner - Investments remains untouched at 194,680.55, keeping every historical date and transaction visible when you click into it.
  • Cumulative Structural Tax Deductions adjusts automatically so your total Assets equal your total Liabilities + Equity down to the penny.

Once you post that Journal Entry, take a look at your main Summary screen:

  • Does Historical Staging Control drop cleanly to 0.00?

Marking the Utility Accounts Inactive

To mark the Historical Capital Staging Account inactive so it never clutters your dropdown menus when recording real payments or receipts, hide both the bank account and its temporary control container.

Step 1: Deactivate the Staging Bank Account

  1. Click on the Bank and Cash Accounts tab on your left sidebar menu.
  2. Click the Edit button next to Historical Capital Staging Account.
  3. Scroll to the bottom of the form and check the box labeled Inactive.
  4. Click Update.

Step 2: Deactivate the Temporary Control Account

  1. Navigate to Settings > Control Accounts.
  2. Click the Edit button next to Historical Staging Control.
  3. Check the Inactive box at the bottom of the form.
  4. Click Update.

Result

Both accounts are now completely hidden from your daily transaction forms and dashboard views. Your historical audit trail remains safely preserved in the database and linked to your Owner - Investments ledger.
