Manager.io - Importing Legacy Transactions via CSV

This page describes the process of importing legacy Capital Inputs for one or more owners (perhaps from a CSV derived from QuickBooks). To see the simple, single 'Starting Balance' approach, jump to the link below.
  • Owner's Capital

You can ingest a CSV of historical inputs to populate your Capital accounts instead of entering a single, flat starting balance.

In double-entry accounting, importing transactions rather than using a flat starting balance is a highly recommended practice. It leaves a bulletproof, historical audit trail directly inside your new ledger. It proves exactly who contributed how much cash and when over the lifetime of your partnership.

To achieve this cleanly without cluttering your main active operational bank feeds, you will use Manager.io's Receipts Import Pipeline routed directly into a specialized structural staging ledger.

Step 1: Format Your Historical Capital Input CSV File

Open Vim, Excel, or a text editor and prepare your historical contribution data as a standard CSV file. Ensure it contains these exact, simple column rows: 

Date,Description,Partner Account,Amount
2014-03-15,Partner Capital Contribution - Partner A,Partner A,15000.00
2014-04-10,Partner Capital Contribution - Partner B,Partner B,15000.00
2016-08-22,Emergency Repair Assessment Input - Partner A,Partner A,4500.00

Step 2: Initialize the Capital Sub-Accounts

  1. Click on Capital Accounts (on the left tabs list - which will appear if 'Capital Accounts' is checked in Customize)
  2. Click on New Capital Account
  3. Name: Enter Partner A's name (e.g., Partner A).
  4. Click Create.
  5. Click New Capital Account again and enter Partner B's name (e.g., Partner B).
  6. Click Create.

Once you create these two partner profiles, Capital accounts will immediately appear in all your transaction and Bank Rule dropdowns.

Subaccounts - Preparing for CSV Data

Before importing, make sure Manager.io has the unique identity profiles for both partners ready to receive the data:

  1. Go to Settings > Capital subaccounts.
  2. Click New Subaccount:
    • Name: Contributions
    • Click Create.
  3. Click New Subaccount:
    • Name: Drawings
    • Click Create.

Understand What Your "Capital Subaccounts" Do Now

Now that your two partners exist in the main Capital Accounts tab, the subaccounts will be available as optional category tags you can pick in a third dropdown whenever you record a capital transaction:

  • Dropdown 1 (Account): Capital accounts
  • Dropdown 2 (Partner): Partner A or Partner B
  • Dropdown 3 (Sub-account): Contributions or Drawings (the items you created in Settings)

Step 3: Set Up Bank Rules

You cannot assign Capital Accounts in bulk directly from the main Receipts or Payments tabs, because Manager.io's Find & recode tool hides Capital Accounts from its dropdown menu.

Instead, you must use Bank Rules combined with the Uncategorized batch-approval screen in Bank and Cash Accounts.

Because you have two partners, you will create four simple Bank Rules (two for Receipts and two for Payments), and then batch-approve all rows in two clicks.

Create the Four Partner Bank Rules

Go to Settings > Bank Rules and create one rule per partner for incoming cash (Receipts) and one per partner for outgoing cash (Payments):

A. Receipt Rules (For Partner Contributions)

  1. Click New Receipt Rule for Partner A:
    • If bank account is: Select your staging or checking account where the rows are waiting.
    • ...and amount is: Any amount
    • ...and description contains: type Partner A's exact name (matching the text in your Description column).
    • Paid by: blank
    • Account: select Capital Accounts
    • Capital Account: select Partner account (Partner's Partner A)
    • Sub-account: select Contributions.
    • Click Create.
  2. Repeat New Receipt Rule for Partner B using Partner B's name in Description, selecting Partner B in Capital account, and selecting Contributions.

B. Payment Rules (For Partner Drawings — if you have outgoing rows)

  1. Click New Payment Rule for Partner A:
    • If bank account is: Select your staging or checking account where the rows are waiting.
    • ...and amount is: Any amount
    • ...and description contains: type Partner A's exact name (matching the text in your Description column).
    • Paid by: blank
    • Account: select Capital Accounts
    • Capital Account: select Partner account (Partner's Partner A)
    • Sub-account: select Contributions.
    • Click Create.
  2. Repeat New Receipt Rule for Partner B using Partner B's name in Description, selecting Partner B in Capital account, and selecting Contributions.

Improving the Summary Display of Capital

To have both Partner's totals displayed as separate items under a Capital Accounts heading

Step 1: Create a Parent Visual Group Under Equity

This creates the bold Capital Accounts header on your Summary screen.

  1. Go to Settings > Chart of Accounts.
  2. Click the New Group button on the left-hand (Balance Sheet) side.
  3. Configure the form:
    • Name: Capital Accounts
    • Group: select Equity
  4. Click Create.

Step 2: Create Two Individual Partner Control Accounts

This splits the combined total into two separate visible lines on your dashboard.

  1. Go to Settings > Control Accounts > Capital Accounts.
  2. Click New Control Account for Partner A:
    • Name: Owner Investment - Partner A (or Partner A's full name)
    • Group: select your new Capital Accounts group.
    • Click Create.
  3. Click New Control Account again for Partner B:
    • Name: Owner Investment - Partner B (or Partner B's full name)
    • Group: select your new Capital Accounts group.
    • Click Create.

Step 3: Re-Link Each Partner Profile to Their Control Account

Now point your existing partner records to their new individual containers.

  1. Click on the Capital Accounts tab on your left sidebar menu.
  2. Click Edit next to Partner A.
  3. Change the Control account dropdown from Capital accounts to Partner A - Capital. Click Update.
  4. Click Edit next to Partner B, change its Control account dropdown to Partner B - Capital, and click Update.

Final Result on Your Summary Page

When you click back to your Summary tab, the default combined Capital accounts line will vanish (since its balance is now zero) and be replaced by your custom nested layout: 

EQUITY
  Capital Accounts
    Partner A - Capital              $ [Partner A Net Total]
    Partner B - Capital              $ [Partner B Net Total]

Note: All of your existing historical receipts, payments, and Contributions/Drawings sub-accounts will automatically follow each partner into their new line without needing to edit any transactions.

Step 4: Batch-Approve the Uncategorized Items

NOTE: For Uncategorized columns to show, click Edit Columns on the Bank and Cash Accounts page, and turn them on.

Now jump over to the Bank and Cash Accounts screen (rather than the Receipts/Payments tabs) to commit the rules in bulk:

  1. Click on Bank and Cash Accounts on your left sidebar menu.
  2. Click the blue numerical link inside the Uncategorized receipts column next to your staging/checking account. (Opening this screen forces Manager.io to scan every waiting row against your four new Bank Rules).
  3. Verify that the rows now show Suspense crossed out and replaced by Capital accounts -> [Partner Name] -> Contributions.
  4. Check the master checkbox to select all matching rows
  5. Click Batch Update.
  6. (If you have outgoing draws) Go back to Bank and Cash Accounts, click the blue link in the Uncategorized payments column, verify the Drawings matches, and click Batch Update.

Verify Your Partner Ledgers

  • Click on the Capital Accounts tab on your left sidebar menu. You will immediately see Partner A and Partner B listed with their exact Contributions, Drawings, and net balances cleanly separated.

Why This Workflow Is Superior to a Flat Starting Balance

  • Permanent Database History: If you drill down into either partner's capital account on the Summary screen, Manager.io will reveal an interactive ledger dating back years. You can search, filter, and sort every single input dollar.
  • Balanced Accounting Migration: Once all receipts are allocated, your Capital Accounts equity rows on the right-hand side of your Profit & Loss will perfectly reflect your true historical capital baselines. Your Historical Capital Staging Account asset balance will perfectly match the cash total injected. You can then cleanly clear out that staging balance against your properties' historical book assets using a single closing Journal Entry!

Step 5. Zero Out the Staging Account (Final Balance Sheet Cleanup)

(Early version of Manager.io used a Journal Entry here, but now Manager.io's Journal Entries cannot touch Bank & Cash Accounts directly.)

You zero out the staging bank account by recording a single Payment directly from that staging account.

How to Zero Out the Staging Account Using a Payment

  1. Click on the Payments tab on your left sidebar menu.
  2. Click the New Payment button at the top.
  3. Configure the payment fields:
    • Date: Set to your migration cutover date (e.g., 2026-09-30 or 2026-01-01).
    • Paid from: select Historical Capital Staging Account (your temporary staging bank account).
    • Description: "Zero out historical capital staging bank account against migration retained earnings"
  4. On the line item grid below:
    • Account: select Retained earnings.
    • Amount: Enter the capital amount (e.g., 48984.72).
  5. Click Create.

Verify Your Summary Screen

When you check in the Bank & Cash Accounts tab:

  • Historical Capital Staging Account (and its control account) will drop to 0.00 and disappear from your Assets.

When you return to your main Summary dashboard take a look at:

  • Retained earnings and click on the blue balance numbers - and you'll see that it will have absorbed the debit offset.

The Owner/Partner's Capital Accounts will remain 100% untouched with all historical transactions intact!

Marking the Utility Accounts Inactive

To mark the Historical Capital Staging Account inactive so it never clutters your dropdown menus when recording real payments or receipts, hide both the bank account and its temporary control container.

Step 1: Deactivate the Staging Bank Account

  1. Click on the Bank and Cash Accounts tab on your left sidebar menu.
  2. Click the Edit button next to Historical Capital Staging Account.
  3. Scroll to the bottom of the form and check the box labeled Inactive.
  4. Click Update.

Step 2: Deactivate the Temporary Control Account

  1. Navigate to Settings > Control Accounts.
  2. Click the Edit button next to Historical Staging Control.
  3. Check the Inactive box at the bottom of the form.
  4. Click Update.

Result

Both accounts are now completely hidden from your daily transaction forms and dashboard views. Your historical audit trail remains safely preserved in the database and linked to your Owner Investments ledger.
