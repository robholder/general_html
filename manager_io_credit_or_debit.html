Manager.io - Credit or Debit?

Master the rules of debits and credits by thinking of them strictly as:

  • "Left Side" (Debit)
  • "Right Side" (Credit)

...of the accounting equation, anchored by the D-E-A-D and C-L-I-C letter map.

Here is a reference guide bringing together the mechanics, the acronym mapping, the bank-statement trap, and real-world rental property scenarios.

1. The Foundation: Why Left Always Equals Right

In double-entry bookkeeping, Debit simply means Left and Credit simply means Right (never think of them as "good" or "bad"). Every account in Manager.io lives on one side of this expanded equation: 

Expenses + Assets + Drawings  =  Liabilities + Income + Capital
\___________________________/    \____________________________/
  LEFT SIDE (Debits Increase)     RIGHT SIDE (Credits Increase)
  • Because every transaction touches at least two accounts and keeps the equation balanced, Total Debits must always equal Total Credits on every single entry.

2. The Complete D-E-A-D / C-L-I-C Reference Map

The first letter of each four-letter word tells you the action (D = Debit, C = Credit). The next three letters tell you the exact account types that go UP when you take that action.

Word 1: D - E - A - D (Accounts That Increase With a DEBIT)

  • D = Debits increase:
    • E = Expenses (Repairs & Maintenance, HOA Fees, Utilities, Taxes & Licenses, Insurance Expense, Management Fees, Interest Paid, Depreciation)
    • A = Assets (Checking, Mortgage Escrow, Property Building Value)
    • D = Drawings (Partner Drawings / Owner Distributions taken out of the business)

Word 2: C - L - I - C (Accounts That Increase With a CREDIT)

  • C = Credits increase:
    • L = Liabilities (Mortgage Loan, Accounts payable, Security Deposits Held)
    • I = Income (Rent Income, Late Fee Income)
    • C = Capital (Partner Capital Contributions, Retained earnings)

3. Why Your Bank Statement Confuses Your Brain

When your bank sends an alert saying "Your account was credited ＄1,800," it uses the exact opposite word from Manager.io:

  • The Bank's Books: To the bank, your checking balance is a Liability (money the bank owes you). Under C-L-I-C, Liabilities go UP with a Credit.
  • Your Books (Manager.io): Cash in checking is your Asset. Under D-E-A-D, Assets go UP with a Debit and go DOWN with a Credit.

4. The 3-Step Method for Any Transaction

Whenever you enter a Journal Entry (or want to understand what Manager.io is doing behind the scenes on a Receipt, Payment, or Purchase Invoice), ask three questions:

  1. Which two (or more) accounts are moving?
  2. Is each account going UP or DOWN?
  3. Apply D-E-A-D or C-L-I-C: Find each account's first letter (E, A, D, L, I, or C). Use the matching action (Debit or Credit) if it is going UP, or use the opposite action if it is going DOWN.

Fastest Shortcut: If cash is involved, start with your bank account first - Cash IN = Debit Bank, Cash OUT = Credit Bank - and the other side automatically takes the opposite column).

Confusing Cash Basis Deposits

QUESTION

If I have rental income and I want to deposit it into my checking account:

  • Rental Income account goes down - and it's income.
  • Cash goes up - and it's an asset.

There is something wrong here. Am I missing something?

ANSWER

Rental Income is NOT a holding bucket of cash that gets drained when you deposit money; it is a running scoreboard of how much rent you earned this year, so it goes UP (not down) when you collect rent.

Here is why your intuition felt like something was off - and why your brain's built-in alarm system was right to pause:

1. Why Rental Income Goes UP (Not Down)

  • Think of Income (Rental Income) like the odometer on your car, not the gas tank:
    • Every time a tenant pays you ＄1,800 in rent, your total rent earned for the year climbs higher (＄0 → ＄1,800 → ＄3,600...).
    • Therefore, Rental Income goes UP (+＄1,800), and under C-L-I-C, Income goes UP with a Credit.
  • At the same time, your Checking account (Cash) goes UP (+＄1,800), and under D-E-A-D, Assets go UP with a Debit.
    Account Account Type (DEAD / CLIC) Direction Debit Credit
    Checking / Cash Asset (DEAD) Up (+＄1,800) ＄1,800.00  
    Rental Income Income (CLIC) Up (+＄1,800)   ＄1,800.00

2. When Does an Account Go DOWN When You Deposit Cash?

Your intuition ("one account goes down while Checking goes up") does happen when you are moving money from another Asset rather than earning new income - for example:

  • Scenario A (Already Billed Earlier): You previously recorded the rent as an unpaid invoice in Accounts Receivable (+＄1,800 Income was already counted then). Now you deposit the check: Checking (Asset) goes UP (Debit) and Accounts Receivable (Asset) goes DOWN (Credit).
  • Scenario B (Undeposited Cash / Property Manager Clearing): You were holding physical cash or a check in an Undeposited Funds asset account, and now you deposit it into Checking: Checking (Asset) goes UP (Debit) and Undeposited Funds (Asset) goes DOWN (Credit).

The Golden Rule: Every time you earn fresh rent, both your Cash Asset and your Rental Income scoreboard go UP together (Debit Asset, Credit Income).

Cash vs Accrual Basis

QUESTION

So how would the paper trail appear for a cash basis scenario where rental income goes up as does the checking account? There must be intermediate steps in the accounting procedure.

ANSWER

In pure cash-basis accounting, there are no intermediate steps - the entire paper trail happens in a single, simultaneous 1-step entry the exact moment the cash or check hits your checking account.

Because cash-basis accounting ignores "earned vs. billed" timelines and only records money when cash physically changes hands, your bank deposit is the trigger that updates both your Checking account (Asset) and your Rental Income scoreboard (Income) at the exact same second.

1. The Complete Cash-Basis Paper Trail (1 Step)

Suppose a tenant sends you a ＄1,800 Zelle transfer or check on November 1, and you deposit it into your Checking account:

  1. Source Document (The Paper Trail):
    • The tenant's check copy, Zelle confirmation, or your bank statement showing +＄1,800.00 on Nov 1.
  2. Manager.io Action:
    • You click Receipts → New Receipt (or import your bank statement and categorize the deposit to Rental Income).
  3. What Manager.io Writes Behind the Scenes (Simultaneous 1-Step Entry):
    Date Account Account Type (DEAD / CLIC) Direction Debit Credit
    Nov 1 Checking - HOLDER AZ Asset (DEAD) Up (+＄1,800) ＄1,800.00  
    Nov 1 Rental Income Income (CLIC) Up (+＄1,800)   ＄1,800.00
  4. Where Those Two Numbers Land on Your Reports:
    • Checking (+＄1,800 Debit) goes straight to your Balance Sheet (showing you now own ＄1,800 more cash).
    • Rental Income (+＄1,800 Credit) goes straight to your Profit & Loss Statement (showing you earned ＄1,800 in rent this year).

2. Why Does It Feel Like There Should Be an Intermediate Step?

Your intuition is picking up on two real-world situations where an intermediate step does exist - neither of which is pure direct cash-basis rent:

  1. You Hold Checks/Cash Before Going to the Bank (Undeposited Funds):
    • Step 1 (Tenant hands you a check on Nov 1): Debit Undeposited Funds (Asset UP) / Credit Rental Income (Income UP).
    • Step 2 (You walk to the bank on Nov 3): Debit Checking (Asset UP) / Credit Undeposited Funds (Asset DOWN).
  2. Accrual Accounting (You Send an Invoice Before Getting Paid):
    • Step 1 (Nov 1 invoice sent): Debit Accounts Receivable (Asset UP) / Credit Rental Income (Income UP).
    • Step 2 (Nov 5 payment arrives): Debit Checking (Asset UP) / Credit Accounts Receivable (Asset DOWN).

Notice that even when you do use those 2-step bridges (Undeposited Funds or Accounts Receivable), Rental Income still only ever goes UP (Credit) in Step 1 - it never goes down! The intermediate account is just a temporary Asset holding tank that goes UP in Step 1 and DOWN in Step 2.

More

In double-entry accounting, equity accounts (including your capital investments) always increase with a credit balance. Entering it as a credit tells the system that you have injected capital into the business, which will offset your mortgage liabilities and turn your equity numbers positive.

Summary of Opening Balance Rules for Your Setup

As you finalize your migration from QuickBooks into Manager.io, you can use this quick reference sheet to ensure every type of account is entered with the correct accounting behavior:

  • Cash / Escrow Bank Accounts: Entered as a Debit (Increases your active cash assets).
  • Property Land Value: Entered as a Debit (Increases your real estate land assets).
  • Fixed Assets (Building Acquisition Cost): Entered as a positive number (Acts as an asset Debit).
  • Fixed Assets (Accumulated Depreciation): Entered as a positive number (Acts as an asset Credit reduction).
  • Mortgage Loan Accounts (Journal Entry): Entered as a Credit (Increases your liabilities/debts).
  • Owner's Capital Accounts (Investment): Entered as a Credit (Increases your business equity).
