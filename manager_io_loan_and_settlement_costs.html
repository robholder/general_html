Moving your books into Manager.io gives you the perfect opportunity to cleanly excise these historical accounting errors.

Fixing in Manager.io

To find your exact annual flat amortization expense, you look at the original cost of the active loan and divide it by the total term length (30 years).

You do not use the remaining book value for this calculation. Because real estate loan costs use straight-line amortization, your tax deduction stays exactly the same dollar amount every single year until the 30-year clock runs out.

Here is the exact calculation for each of your properties based on your QuickBooks records:

  1. Sawtooth Refinance Amortization • Total Original Refinance Cost: \(2,220.50 (Loan Cost) + \)756.18 (Settlement Cost) = $2,976.68 • The Math: \(\frac{\\(2,976.68}{\text{30\ Years}}=\\)99.2226\) • Your Annual Sawtooth Deduction: $99.22 per year.

Explanation

In real estate accounting, Loan Costs and Settlement/Closing Costs cannot simply be lumped into your properties as general expenses. They are governed by strict financial rules and must be treated as separate line items on your Balance Sheet. Since you are closing out your QuickBooks history, it is vital to understand exactly what these lines represent so we can transfer their remaining values into Manager.io correctly without losing your historical tax deductions. Here is a breakdown of what these accounts are and how they must be handled in your new system:

  1. Loan Costs & Amortization (Purchase & Refinance)

What this is: These are the fees you paid to your lenders to secure your mortgages (e.g., loan origination fees, underwriting fees, and appraisal fees). • The Tax Rule: The IRS forbids you from expensing loan costs all at once in the year you buy or refinance a property. Instead, you must spread the cost out evenly over the life of the loan (e.g., 30 years, which is why your QuickBooks notes say Amort30). • The QuickBooks Setup: Your legacy file has a positive asset account tracking the original fee size, and a matching negative account (Amort30) tracking how much of that fee you have successfully written off over the years.

How to Migrate This to Manager.io:

Because these represent an intangible asset that you are actively writing off, you will create two standard accounts inside your Chart of Accounts for each loan:

  1. Create the Accounts globally (Settings > Chart of Accounts): • Unamortized Loan Costs - Sawtooth (Asset Group, Investing activities Cash Flow) • Loan Cost Amortization Expense (Expense Group)
  2. Initialize the Balances (Settings > Starting Balances > Balance Sheet Accounts): • Take the original loan cost asset value from QuickBooks and subtract the cumulative Amort30 negative value. • Enter that remaining, unwritten-off net value into Manager.io as a Debit starting balance for that property's loan cost asset.

Every year moving forward, you will write a simple manual Journal Entry to move 1/30th of that original fee out of your asset account and into your Loan Cost Amortization Expense account to claim your annual tax deduction.

  1. Settlement Costs - Purchase & Refinance

What this is: These are the legal and transfer fees paid at the closing table that were not directly charged by the lender (e.g., title insurance, escrow agent fees, recording fees, and transfer taxes). • The Purchase Settlement Costs Rule: These costs are considered part of the long-term investment required to acquire the property. They must be added directly to your permanent property cost basis. They do not get their own ongoing account. • The Refinance Settlement Costs Rule: Closing costs on a refinance are legally treated exactly like Loan Costs. They must be amortized over the life of the new mortgage loan rather than added to the building's value.

How to Migrate This to Manager.io:

• For the Purchase Settlement Costs: Check your building's Acquisition Cost starting balance in Manager.io. If these historical closing costs were sitting in a separate account in QuickBooks, add them straight into your building's Acquisition Cost or your Property Land Value inside Manager.io. They will permanently increase your depreciable foundation. • For the Refinance Settlement Costs: Combine these numbers directly with your Refinance Loan Costs asset balance described above and amortize them together over the remaining years left on that mortgage.

⚠️ A Critical Red Flag to Check for Refinanced Loans

If a property was refinanced years ago, the original Purchase Loan Costs from the first mortgage should have been wiped out completely. When you refinance a rental property, any remaining unamortized loan costs from your old, dead loan can legally be expensed 100% in full on your taxes in the year the refinance happens. If your old QuickBooks file is still holding onto unamortized loan cost lines for an original purchase loan that was later replaced by a refinance, that is an accounting error that should be completely cleared to zero before moving to Manager.io.
