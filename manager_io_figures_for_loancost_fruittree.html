Loan Cost Amortization - Fruit Tree

Assuming a standard 30-year (360-month) loan term starting on January 1, 2011, your January 1, 2026 Starting Balance is ＄1,045.18, and your 2026 Annual Amortization is ＄69.68.

Because exactly 15 full years (180 months) elapsed between January 1, 2011, and December 31, 2025, half of the original ＄2,090.36 cost belongs to prior years. The remaining half carries forward into Manager.io for the next 15 years.

Amortization Schedule Breakdown (30-Year Term)

Milestone / Metric Calculation Amount
Original Loan Cost (2011-01-01) Total capitalized closing fees ＄2,090.36
Monthly Amortization ＄2,090.36 ÷ 360 months ＄5.81
Annual Amortization (Full Year) ＄2,090.36 ÷ 30 years ＄69.68
Prior Years Elapsed (2011–2025) 15 years × ＄69.6787 ＄1,045.18
Manager.io Starting Balance (2026-01-01) ＄2,090.36 − ＄1,045.18 (15 yrs left) ＄1,045.18
2026 Year-End Amortization (2026-12-31) Full year deduction for 2026 ＄69.68
Ending Asset Balance (2026-12-31) ＄1,045.18 − ＄69.68 ＄975.50

Note: If this was a 15-year mortgage instead of a 30-year mortgage, the loan costs fully expired on December 31, 2025, leaving a ＄0.00 balance for 2026.

How to Enter These Figures into Manager.io

  1. Set the 2026-01-01 Starting Balance.
    • Go to Settings > Starting Balances > Balance Sheet Accounts and click New Starting Balance.
    • Select Unamortized Loan Costs - [Property Name], set Type to Debit, and enter 1045.18.
    • Manager.io automatically absorbs the missing ＄1,045.18 difference into Retained earnings to account for the 15 prior years.
  2. Record the 2026 Annual Write-Off.
    • Go to Journal Entries > New Journal Entry dated 2026-12-31.
    • Debit Loan Cost Amortization Expense for 69.68 (assigned to your property's Division).
    • Credit Unamortized Loan Costs - [Property Name] for 69.68 (assigned to your property's Division).

Important Tax Note on Catching Up Missed Years

  • Do not lump all ＄1,114.86 into 2026 Expense. Under IRS "allowed or allowable" rules, you cannot claim 15 years of missed amortization as a regular current-year expense on your 2026 Schedule E.
  • How to claim the missed ＄1,045.18. On your 2026 return, claim only ＄69.68 as your regular annual amortization. To recover the missed ＄1,045.18 from 2011–2025, have your CPA attach IRS Form 3115 (Application for Change in Accounting Method) to take a one-time Section 481(a) catch-up deduction without amending 15 years of past tax returns.

Recurring Deduction Prompts

To set up an automated year-end reminder in Manager.io so it prompts you every December 31 to post your ＄69.68 amortization write-off, use the Recurring Journal Entries feature. When a recurring schedule hits its due date, Manager.io displays a yellow notification banner at the top of your Journal Entries tab so you can post it in one click.

Step 1: Enable Recurring Journal Entries

  1. Go to Settings on your left sidebar menu and click Recurring Transactions (or Recurring Journal Entries).
  2. (If you do not see it under Settings) Click Customize at the bottom of your left sidebar, check Recurring Journal Entries, and click Update.

Step 2: Create the Annual Amortization Schedule

  1. Open Recurring Journal Entries and click New Recurring Journal Entry.
  2. Configure the schedule settings:
    • Next issue date: Set to 2026-12-31.
    • Interval: Select Every 1 Year(s).
    • End date: Set to 2040-12-31 (or leave blank and delete it after the final year).
    • Narration: Annual Loan Cost Amortization - [Property Name]
  3. Build the 2-line grid below:
    Line Account Description Debit Credit Division
    1 Loan Cost Amortization Expense Annual 30-yr loan cost tax deduction 69.68   [Property Name]
    2 Unamortized Loan Costs - [Property Name] Reduces remaining unamortized asset balance   69.68 [Property Name]
  4. Click Create.

How the Prompt Works Each Year

  • On December 31: A yellow banner reading 1 recurring journal entry due will appear at the top of your Journal Entries tab (and in your left sidebar counter).
  • One-Click Posting: Click the banner, verify the date (YYYY-12-31), and click Create. Manager.io immediately posts the ＄69.68 deduction and automatically advances the next reminder to December 31 of the following year.
  • (Tip: You can add your Annual Property Depreciation lines into this exact same Recurring Journal Entry so one single year-end prompt posts both of your non-cash tax deductions at the same time).

Combine Loan Coast Amortization and Annual Property Depreciation

To combine both your Loan Cost Amortization and Annual Property Depreciation into a single year-end prompt, add two more lines to that same Recurring Journal Entry.

Because both schedules run every December 31, bundling them into one 4-line template ensures you only have to click Create once at the end of every year to record all non-cash tax deductions for the property.

How to Bundle Depreciation into Your Recurring Journal Entry

  1. Go to Settings > Recurring Journal Entries (or click Recurring Journal Entries in your left sidebar).
  2. Click Edit next to your Annual Loan Cost Amortization - [Property Name] template.
  3. Update the Narration to reflect both deductions:
    • Annual Year-End Depreciation & Loan Cost Amortization - [Property Name]
  4. Keep Lines 1 and 2 as they are, and add Lines 3 and 4 below them:
    Line Account Description Debit Credit Division
    1 Loan Cost Amortization Expense Annual 30-yr loan cost tax deduction 69.68   [Property Name]
    2 Unamortized Loan Costs - [Property Name] Reduces remaining unamortized asset balance   69.68 [Property Name]
    3 Annual Property Depreciation Annual 27.5-yr building tax deduction [Annual Depr ＄]   [Property Name]
    4 Accumulated Depreciation - [Property Name] Increases contra-asset offset on Balance Sheet   [Annual Depr ＄] [Property Name]
  5. Check the End date field:
    • Because a 27.5-year depreciation schedule ends in 2038 (for a January 1, 2011 purchase) while your 30-year loan amortization runs through 2040, leave the End date blank so the prompt continues through 2040. (In 2039 and 2040, you will simply delete Lines 3 and 4 from the template once the building is fully depreciated).
  6. Click Update.

What Happens on December 31, 2026

  • A single yellow notification banner (1 recurring journal entry due) will appear at the top of your Journal Entries tab.
  • Clicking it opens the pre-filled 4-line entry dated 2026-12-31. Click Create, and Manager.io simultaneously updates your Balance Sheet assets and posts both tax deductions to your 2026 Profit & Loss statement.
