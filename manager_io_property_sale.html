Accounting For Property Sale

Recording the sale of a rental property is the ultimate final test for your ledger. When you sell a property, you aren't just logging a large cash deposit; you are performing an accounting liquidation. Every single asset, liability, and contra-asset account tied to that property division must be systematically wiped out and brought to exactly ＄0.00.

To ensure your accounts are perfectly accurate and ready for your tax preparer to complete IRS Form 4797 (Sale of Business Property) and Schedule D, you must execute a strict, four-step accounting protocol.

Step 1: Bring Depreciation and Amortization Up to Date

Before closing the books on the sale date, you must record a mid-year or partial-year depreciation and amortization entry. If you sell the property on June 30th, you must claim exactly 6 months' worth of write-offs so your accumulated expenses are completely accurate up to the day of closing.

  1. Go to the Depreciation Entries tab
  2. Click New Depreciation Entry.
  3. Set the date to the exact Closing Date of the sale.
  4. Calculate and log the pro-rated amount for that specific division:
    • Building Depreciation: (Annual Depreciation ÷ 12) × Months owned this year.
    • Loan Amortization: (Annual Amortization ÷ 12) × Months owned this year.

Step 2: Establish a Temporary "Property Disposal" Clearing Account

To prevent your main Profit & Loss statement from looking chaotic with multi-hundred-thousand-dollar lines, professionals use a temporary clearing account to flush out the balances.

  1. Go to Settings > Chart of Accounts > New Account.
  2. Name it Property Disposal Clearing Account and place it in the Assets group.
  3. Click Create.

Step 3: The Liquidation Journal Entry

You will use a single, comprehensive Journal Entry backdated to the sale date. This entry strips the property out of your sub-ledgers and traps the raw financial gain or loss inside your clearing account.

Assuming you are selling Sawtooth, your Journal Entry will look exactly like this:

Account Type Account Name Division Debit Credit Purpose / Description
Fixed Asset Sawtooth - Accumulated Depreciation Sawtooth ＄112,372.00   Empties out the negative depreciation bucket
Liability Mortgage Loan - Sawtooth Sawtooth ＄118,041.06   Wipes out the remaining mortgage debt principal
Standard Asset Property Disposal Clearing Account Sawtooth ＄[Plugging Total]   Temporary landing spot for the remaining asset weight
Fixed Asset Sawtooth - Building Value (Cost) Sawtooth   ＄153,920.00 Wipes the original building card down to ＄0.00
Standard Asset Property Land Value - Sawtooth Sawtooth   ＄51,302.00 Wipes the raw land ledger down to ＄0.00
Standard Asset Unamortized Loan Costs - Sawtooth Sawtooth   ＄1,861.11 Flushes out the remaining unamortized financing costs

Note on the "Plugging Total": Your Debits and Credits must match perfectly. You will add up the right column, subtract the left column, and type the difference into the Property Disposal Clearing Account Debit line.

Step 4: Record the Net Closing Wire Deposit

When the final wire transfer from the escrow/title company hits your checking account, you do not record it as rental income.

  1. Go to your Bank and Cash Accounts tab
  2. Click New Receipt inside your Checking Account.
  3. Set the date to the Closing Settlement day.
  4. Under the Account column, select Property Disposal Clearing Account and tag the Sawtooth division.
  5. Type the Net Cash Wire Amount received from your settlement sheet.
  6. Click Create.

The Final Tax Revelation

Once Step 4 is finished, open your Chart of Accounts and look at the balance of your Property Disposal Clearing Account:

  • If it has a Credit Balance: This represents your Realized Capital Gain on the sale.
  • If it has a Debit Balance: This represents your Realized Capital Loss.

You can then create a permanent Equity or *Income8 account called Gain/Loss on Sale of Real Estate and write one final journal entry to move that balance out of your assets and onto your Profit & Loss statement.

Crucial Protocols for Your Tax Preparer

When you hand this file to your CPA, provide them with the HUD-1 Settlement Statement or Closing Disclosure Form alongside your Manager.io reports. They will use your clean ledger to isolate:

  1. Unamortized Loan Costs Write-off: The ＄1,861.11 you flushed out in Step 3 automatically hits your final tax return as a 100% ordinary business expense deduction, shielding your final year's rental income.
  2. Section 1250 Depreciation Recapture: Your CPA will look at your accumulated depreciation balance to calculate your recapture tax rate (capped at 25%), while your land and building cost eliminations will cleanly establish your long-term capital gains boundary.

Step-by-step Example

To see exactly how this liquidation protocol functions, let's run through a realistic, step-by-step example.

Let's assume you sell the Sawtooth property on November 30, 2026, for a gross contract contract sale price of ＄250,000.00. After the title company subtracts your outstanding mortgage principal and closing fees, they wire a net cash payment of ＄121,570.83 directly into your business checking account.

Here is exactly how the numbers flow through your four-step protocol to isolate your capital gains automatically on your dashboard:

Step 1: Bring Your 2026 Write-Offs Up to Date (Pro-Rated)

Since you owned the property for exactly 10 months in 2026 (Jan 1 to Oct 31) before selling it in November, you must claim 10 months of pro-rated straight-line deductions so your book values match the closing day reality:

  • Partial Building Depreciation: (＄5,597.00 / 12) * 10 = ＄4,664.17
  • Partial Loan Cost Amortization: (＄99.22 / 12) * 10 = ＄82.68

You post a Depreciation Entry dated 2026-11-30 for these amounts. This brings your cumulative, historical Accumulated Depreciation bucket to exactly ＄111,439.21 (＄106,775.04 + ＄4,664.17) and your remaining Unamortized Loan Costs asset down to exactly ＄1,778.43 (＄1,861.11 - ＄82.68).

Step 2: Create the Property Disposal Clearing Account

As detailed prior, ensure you have your temporary standard asset row named Property Disposal Clearing Account active inside your Chart of Accounts.

Step 3: The Liquidation Journal Entry

On the closing date (2026-11-30), you post this comprehensive Journal Entry to strip the asset profiles out of your system and compute the net "paper weight" of the property:

Account Type Account Name Division Debit Credit Purpose / Memo
Fixed Asset Sawtooth - Accumulated Depreciation Sawtooth ＄111,439.21   Empties out the 19.8-year write-off bucket
Liability Mortgage Loan - Sawtooth Sawtooth ＄118,041.06   Wipes the remaining loan debt principal to ＄0
Standard Asset Property Land Value - Sawtooth Sawtooth   ＄51,302.00 Wipes the raw land ledger down to ＄0
Fixed Asset Sawtooth - Building Value (Cost) Sawtooth   ＄153,920.00 Wipes original building card down to ＄0
Standard Asset Unamortized Loan Costs - Sawtooth Sawtooth   ＄1,778.43 Flushes remaining unamortized financing costs
Standard Asset Property Disposal Clearing Account Sawtooth   ＄97,520.16 [The Plugging Balance]

How to calculate the Plugging Balance:

  • Total up the Credits column (＄207,000.43).
  • Subtract the two known Debits (＄111,439.21 + ＄118,041.06).
  • The remaining difference of ＄97,520.16 is typed directly into the Clearing Account row to make the entry balance perfectly to zero.

Step 4: Record the Net Closing Wire Deposit

When you log the real-world title wire transfer hitting your bank register, you process a New Receipt inside your Checking account:

  • Date: 2026-11-30
  • Account: Property Disposal Clearing Account | Tag Sawtooth Division
  • Amount: ＄121,570.83 (The net cash wire from your closing sheet)

The Final Tax Revelation on Your Dashboard

The moment Step 4 is posted, go to your Chart of Accounts and look at the balance of your Property Disposal Clearing Account. The system has automatically performed your tax calculations behind the scenes: 

   ＄121,570.83  (Credit - Net Cash Wire Received from Step 4)
-   ＄97,520.16  (Debit - Internal System Paper Weight from Step 3)
------------------------------------------------------------------
=   ＄24,050.67  (Net Credit Balance Remaining in the Account)

Because the account holds a remaining Credit balance of ＄24,050.67, Manager.io is telling you that you have achieved a Realized Capital Gain of exactly ＄24,050.67 on the sale!

What You Hand Your CPA

When tax season arrives, your reports will provide your tax preparer with a pristine audit trail. They can fill out your tax returns using your exact figures:

  1. Ordinary Income Expense: Your pro-rated 2026 loan amortization (＄82.68) and the flushed remainder (＄1,778.43) are captured as normal write-offs.
  2. Depreciation Recapture: Your CPA sees that you have ＄111,439.21 sitting in accumulated depreciation, which will be taxed at your standard recapture rate (capped at 25%). See Below
  3. Capital Gains: Your ledger's calculated ＄24,050.67 gain maps directly onto Schedule D, ensuring your file reconciles with absolute accuracy.

Recapture Rate (capped at 25%)

In the United States, the standard Section 1250 Depreciation Recapture tax rate is not a single fixed percentage, but it is strictly capped at a maximum rate of 25%.

When you sell a rental property for a profit, the IRS wants to "take back" (recapture) the tax breaks you enjoyed over the years from your annual building depreciation deductions.

Instead of taxing this portion of your profit at the lower, standard long-term capital gains rates (which are usually 0%, 15%, or 20%), the IRS applies a specialized rule:

How Your Recapture Rate is Determined

Your actual depreciation recapture tax rate is determined by matching it to your ordinary income tax bracket up to that 25% structural ceiling:

  • If your personal ordinary tax bracket is 12% or 22%: Your depreciation recapture rate will match your bracket exactly (12% or 22%)
  • If your personal ordinary tax bracket is 24%, 32%, 35%, or 37%: Your depreciation recapture rate stops climbing and hits the absolute legal ceiling of 25%.

The Two-Tier Tax Breakdown for Your Example

Using the mock sale numbers we calculated for your Sawtooth property, your total profit would be split into two completely separate tax buckets on your tax return:

  1. The Depreciation Recapture Bucket (＄111,439.21): Because you wrote off this amount over 19.8 years, this portion of your gain is taxed at your ordinary tax bracket rate, up to the maximum cap of 25%.
  2. The True Capital Gains Bucket (＄24,050.67): Because this profit represents pure asset growth above what you paid for it, this portion escapes the recapture penalty and is taxed at the much more favorable long-term capital gains rate (typically 15% for most real estate investors).

This two-tier calculation is exactly why keeping your original building costs and your accumulated depreciation values in two strictly separate accounts inside Manager.io is so vital. It allows your tax preparer to see the dividing line between these two buckets instantly, ensuring you are never overtaxed when you close the sale.

QUESTION

What if the property is held in a Partnership (that issues K1s)? How is the Depreciation bucket handled for each partner?

ANSWER

When a rental property is held inside a Partnership (which files a Form 1065 and issues K-1s), the depreciation bucket is handled completely differently than a sole proprietorship.

The property's accumulated depreciation bucket is never split or divided inside Manager.io.

The partnership entity itself owns the property and tracks the entire ＄111,439.21 accumulated depreciation total as a single global line item on its corporate Balance Sheet.

The breakdown across the individual owners happens strictly on paper at tax time via the Schedule K-1 allocation process. Here is exactly how that mechanism operates:

1. How the Annual Depreciation Flow Works

Every year, Manager.io records the global depreciation expense (e.g., ＄5,597.00) on the partnership's Profit & Loss statement.

  • When the partnership files its annual Form 1065 tax return, this depreciation expense drops the company's net ordinary rental income.
  • The tax software then automatically splits that net income (or loss) according to your partnership agreement percentages (e.g., 50/50).
  • Each partner receives a Schedule K-1 showing their personal share of that year's net rental income, which has already been reduced by their share of the depreciation.

2. What Happens to the Depreciation Bucket Upon Sale?

When the partnership sells the property, the entity handles the multi-step liquidation protocol in Manager.io exactly like the mock example we ran through. The partnership's tax software calculates the total ＄111,439.21 depreciation recapture and the ＄24,050.67 capital gain at the corporate level.

The partnership then passes these gains out to the individual partners on their final Schedule K-1 forms:

  • Box 9c (Recapture): Each partner is issued their specific percentage share of the Section 1250 Depreciation Recapture (e.g., if you are 50/50 partners, your K-1 will report ＄55,719.60 of recapture).
  • Box 9a (Net Long-Term Capital Gain): Each partner is issued their share of the pure capital profit (e.g., ＄12,025.33).

The individual partners then copy those specific K-1 boxes straight onto their personal Form 1040 tax returns. Each partner's personal ordinary tax bracket then determines whether their individual recapture rate hits the maximum 25% ceiling or stays lower.

3. The Partner's "Outside Basis" Tracker (Inside Equity)

While you don't split the property asset rows in Manager.io, you do split the owner equity impact.

Under your Capital Accounts module in Manager.io, you will have a separate profile row for each partner (e.g., Capital Account - Partner A and Capital Account - Partner B).

When the property is sold, the final profit generated inside your Property Disposal Clearing Account is transferred into your equity section using a closing journal entry:

  • You Credit each partner's capital account for their specific percentage share of the total gain.
  • This increases their individual equity tracking balances, giving your partnership ledger a flawless, audit-ready map of exactly how much cash each partner is legally entitled to withdraw when you distribute the cash proceeds from the sale!
