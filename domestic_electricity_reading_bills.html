SCE - Reading the Bill

Southern California Edison (SCE) solar bills look confusing because you are billed under Net Energy Metering (NEM) - meaning your bill tracks what you pull from the grid minus what your solar panels send back, split into two main parts: Delivery (poles and wires) and Generation (the actual power).

Using your November 2026 bill (＄51.53) as a guide, here is a plain-English walkthrough of every section that actually matters.

1. Page 1: The "Bottom Line" Snapshot

  • Amount Due (＄51.53) & Due Date (11/23/26): The actual check you need to pay this month.
  • Net Energy Metering (NEM) Month (Month 1 of 12): Because you have solar, SCE tracks your energy over a 12-month "Relevant Period". November is Month 1 of your annual cycle.
  • Current Month's Net kWh (25 kWh): Your total electricity pulled from SCE minus the excess solar electricity you pushed back to SCE. A positive number means you used more than you sent back; a negative number (like -36 kWh on your Nov 2025 bill) means your solar exported more than you pulled from the grid.

2. Page 4 (Top): Your Usage & Time-of-Day Table

Because you are on the TOU-D-4-9PM (Time-of-Use) rate plan, SCE charges different prices depending on when you use power:

  • Mid Peak (4 PM – 9 PM): The most expensive window (when solar stops producing and everyone turns on lights/appliances).
  • Off Peak (9 PM – 8 AM): Medium-priced overnight hours.
  • Super Off Peak (8 AM – 4 PM): The cheapest window - and when your solar panels are producing the most.

How to read the "Electricity (kWh)" box on Page 4:

  • Consumption (217 kWh total): Power your home pulled from SCE when your solar wasn't covering it (e.g., at night).
  • Net Generation (-192 kWh total): Extra solar power your panels sent back to SCE's grid.
  • Total (25 kWh): 217 kWh pulled − 192 kWh sent back = 25 kWh net.

3. Pages 4 & 5: "Details of Your New Charges" (How the ＄51.53 is Built)

Every SCE bill splits your charges into Delivery (getting power to your house) and Generation (making the power). You pay both rates on every kWh.

4. Page 6: "Details of Your Tracked Charges" (Why It Looks Like a Duplicate!)

This is the section that confuses almost every solar homeowner because it lists the exact same kWh lines a second time:

  • Why it's there: Depending on your NEM version (NEM 1.0/2.0 vs. NEM 3.0 / Monthly Billing), SCE tracks your pure energy charges vs. solar credits (＄24.54 on Page 6) in a running 12-month bucket so it can calculate whether you owe money or get a Net Surplus payout at Month 12.
  • What you actually need to look at: Look at the small table at the bottom of Page 6 ("Monthly summary of your tracked credits and charges"). As long as you pay the "Amount due" on Page 1 (＄51.53), you do not pay Page 6 separately - it is already included in Page 1's total.

3 Quick Rules of Thumb When Scanning Any Future SCE Bill

  1. Check Page 1 (Current Month's net kWh): Negative = your solar produced more than you used; Positive = you used more than your solar produced.
  2. Check Page 4 (Consumption vs. Net Generation): Look at Mid Peak (4 PM – 9 PM). Every kWh you use between 4 PM and 9 PM costs roughly ＄0.50/kWh (＄0.31 delivery + ＄0.19 generation), whereas power used before 4 PM is covered directly by your solar panels before it even hits the meter.
  3. Expect a ~＄24 Floor Every Month: Even if your net kWh is zero or negative, your monthly bill will almost always be around ＄24–＄26 because of the new ＄0.79/day Base Services Charge plus Nonbypassable Charges.

NEM Status

Based on your bills, you are on NEM 2.0 (Net Energy Metering 2.0) with monthly billing - which is a major advantage because SCE credits you at near-full retail rates (roughly ＄0.31–＄0.36/kWh) for every excess solar kWh you send back to the grid.

Here is what NEM 2.0 actually means for your wallet, how your credits work across the year, and how to get the most out of it.

1. How We Know You Are on NEM 2.0 (and Why It Matters)

  • Full Retail Export Credits: On Page 4 of your Nov 2026 bill, your excess solar exported during Super Off-Peak (-147 kWh) was credited at ＄0.31078/kWh (＄0.24390 delivery + ＄0.06688 generation). Under California's newer NEM 3.0 (Solar Billing Plan), daytime solar exports only earn pennies (~＄0.04–＄0.08/kWh). Your NEM 2.0 status locks in high retail credit values for 20 years from the date your solar system was originally turned on (and transfers to the next buyer if you ever sell the home).
  • Nonbypassable Charges (NBCs): The "2.0" rule is why you see a separate line on Page 4 for Nonbypassable Charges (194 kWh × ＄0.01164 = ＄2.26). Under NEM 2.0, you pay those small public-purpose/wildfire fees on every kWh you pull from the grid, even if your solar exports offset your energy charges.

2. How Your 12-Month NEM Cycle Works ("Relevant Period")

Even though SCE bills you every month (instead of once a year at a giant "True-Up"), your solar credits still operate on a 12-month cycle starting in November (Month 1) and ending in October (Month 12):

  1. Banking Credits in Spring & Summer (Months 4–10): During sunny spring/summer months when your panels produce far more than you use, your bill will show negative net kWh and build up a dollar credit balance on Page 6 ("Year-to-date energy credits").
  2. Spending Credits in Fall & Winter (Months 11–3): When days are shorter and you pull more power from SCE (like November's +25 kWh), SCE automatically draws down your banked dollar credits to cover your energy charges.
  3. End of Month 12 (October True-Up / Net Surplus Compensation):
    • If you end Month 12 with leftover credits (negative Year-to-Date kWh): Because your bill lists Net Surplus Compensation (NSC) option: Rollover (Page 5), SCE converts any excess kWh you generated over the full year at the wholesale rate (~＄0.04–＄0.08/kWh) and rolls that dollar credit forward into Month 1 of your next year.
    • What credits NEVER cover: Your solar credits can only pay for Energy Charges (Delivery + Generation). They cannot pay for the new ~＄23/month Base Services Charge or Nonbypassable Charges (~＄2–＄4/month) - those are billed and paid monthly.

3. The Golden Rule of NEM 2.0: "Buy Low, Sell High"

Because you are on Time-of-Use (TOU-D-4-9PM), every kWh is not equal:

Time Window Hours What 1 kWh Costs / Credits You Best Strategy
Super Off-Peak 8:00 AM – 4:00 PM ~＄0.31 / kWh Run heavy appliances now (dishwasher, laundry, pool pump, EV charging, pre-cooling AC). Your solar covers it directly, or it only costs/credits ＄0.31/kWh.
Off-Peak 9:00 PM – 8:00 AM ~＄0.35 / kWh Safe window for overnight appliances once peak pricing ends at 9:00 PM.
Mid-Peak (Expensive!) 4:00 PM – 9:00 PM ~＄0.50 / kWh Avoid heavy electricity use. Solar production drops off after 4 PM, so every kWh you pull during these 5 hours eats up 1.6 kWh of your daytime solar credits (＄0.50 ÷ ＄0.31).
