Usage - Power Meter

To calculate the monthly electrical cost of a specific electronic device or appliance, you need to find its kilowatt-hour (kWh) consumption using your power meter and multiply it by your local electricity rate.

The average residential electricity rate in Ontario, CA is approximately $0.31 per kWh.

Method 1: The Cumulative Time Test (Most Accurate)

Devices like refrigerators or air conditioners don't pull a constant amount of power—they cycle on and off. Leaving your power meter attached for a multi-day test captures this real-world cycling.

  1. Leave the meter plugged in for exactly 24 hours (or a full week for more precision).
  2. Find the total cumulative kWh on the display screen.
  3. Calculate daily usage by dividing that total kWh by the number of days you ran the test.
  4. Multiply by 30 days to project your total monthly kWh.
  5. Multiply by $0.31 (or your exact utility rate) to get your monthly cost.

Method 2: The Quick Calculation (For Constant Loads)

If you are measuring something that pulls a stable, predictable amount of power whenever it is turned on (like a lamp or a gaming console), you can estimate the cost instantly using the live Watts (W) reading:

Monthly Cost = Monthly kWh * Electricity Rate ($/kWh)

Breakdown

Formula: Cost = ((Watts * Hours * 30) / 1000) * Rate

Variables:

  • Watts : Current real-time power draw of the device
  • Hours : Average hours the device runs per day
  • 30 : Days in a standard billing month
  • 1000 : Conversion factor from Watts to Kilowatts (kW)
  • Rate : Ontario, CA utility rate (~$0.31 per kWh)

Example: Device pulling 150W running 8 hours a day: Cost = ((150 * 8 * 30) / 1000) * 0.31 

= (36000 / 1000) * 0.31
= 36 * 0.31
= $11.16 per month
