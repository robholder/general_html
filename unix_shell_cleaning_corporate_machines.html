Cleaning Accounts Before Deactivation

Cleaning both a macOS client (for Anyware remote working) and the remote Linux account.

To securely clear your personal files, caches, and traces before leaving your job, you must target both your local macOS laptop and your remote Linux workstation accessed via Anyware (PCoIP).

Since these are corporate assets, your goal is to destroy your personal/working data without breaking the underlying operating system or violating company offboarding policies.

Part 1: Clearing Your Remote Linux Workstation

If you've stored a Vimwiki repo and potentially other files to /var/tmp, those files will not be wiped by an IT account deactivation since they sit on the local scratch disk. You must destroy them manually.

1. Securely Shred Local & Temporary Files

Standard rm only deletes the pointer to a file, leaving the data salvageable. Use shred to overwrite the data blocks of your sensitive local directories before deleting them:

Shred and delete your local Vimwiki notes completely

find /var/tmp/vimwiki -type f -exec shred -u -z -n 3 {} +
rm -rf /var/tmp/vimwiki
# Re-create the symlink to a nonexistent target so it doesn't break terminal startups
ln -sf /dev/null ~/vimwiki

2. Wipe Git Credentials & SSH Keys

Remove your local credentials and identities so no one can impersonate your git signature or hop into other servers: 

rm -rf ~/.ssh/*
rm -f ~/.gitconfig
git config --global --unset-all user.name
git config --global --unset-all user.email

3. Wipe Your Crontab and Shell Histories

Remove the touch cronjob we created and erase your command history, which contains records of files you edited and commands you ran: 

# Clear all crontab entries instantly
crontab -r
# Wipe bash/zsh command histories
history -c && history -w
rm -f ~/.bash_history ~/.zsh_history ~/.viminfo

Part 2: Clearing Your macOS Client

On macOS, your focus is clearing browser data, local app caches, and any lingering keys stored on the local hardware.

1. Deauthorize & Sign Out of Core Apps

Before deleting files, sever the cloud connections to prevent corporate data from syncing backward:

  • Browsers (Chrome/Safari/Firefox): Go to settings, sign out of your profile, and select "Delete profile data from this device." Then clear all time history, cookies, and cache.
  • Slack / Teams: Sign out of all corporate workspaces and reset the app cache via the application menus.
  • Apple Account (if applicable): System Settings > Click your name > Sign Out.

2. Clean the Local Keychain & SSH Keys

Macs aggressively cache passwords. Open Terminal on your Mac and run: 

# Remove local SSH keys
rm -rf ~/.ssh/*
# Clear command line histories
history -c; rm -f ~/.bash_history ~/.zsh_history ~/.lesshst

Open the Keychain Access app via Spotlight, search for your personal or enterprise Git tokens (like GitHub/GitLab tokens), right-click, and select Delete.

3. Clear the Tmp and Downloads Folders

Mac users often leave files in local staging grounds: 

rm -rf ~/Downloads/*
rm -rf ~/.Trash/*

Part 3: What to Leave Untouched

  • Do not wipe your network home directory (~/): Your company will back this up or transfer ownership to your manager. Deleting the root of your home directory can trigger automated IT alerts.
  • Do not use sudo rm -rf /: Wiping the OS system files will flag the machine as "broken" or "tampered with" in the corporate MDM (Mobile Device Management) system, creating an unnecessary headache with corporate security.

Dotfiles Configs

To safely and completely remove an entire ecosystem of dotfile configurations without breaking your active shell while you are doing it, you need a method that cleanly targets and destroys the symbolic links in your home directory, followed by erasing the repository itself.

Since your configurations are probably heavily symlinked, standard file deletion might leave behind broken "dead" shortcuts (orphaned symlinks) scattered all over your home folder.

Here is the most precise, secure way to clean them out on both your Mac and Linux systems.

Step 1: Find and Destroy All Symlinks Pointing to Your Repo

Instead of hunting for symlinks manually, you can use the find command to look specifically inside your home directory (~) for any symbolic links that point directly into your dotfiles repository folder.

Assuming your repository is named dotfiles and sits in your home directory (~/dotfiles), run this command: 

find ~ -maxdepth 3 -type l -lname "$HOME/dotfiles/*" -verbose -delete

How this command safely does the heavy lifting:

  • -maxdepth 3: Restricts the search to your home directory and immediate subdirectories (like ~/.config/), preventing it from wandering too deep into system files.
  • -type l: Tells the system to explicitly look only for symbolic links.
  • -lname "$HOME/dotfiles/*": Filters the search so it only targets symlinks whose targets live inside your dotfiles repository. This guarantees you won't accidentally delete your Vimwiki link or other unrelated system shortcuts.
  • -verbose -delete: Prints the name of every single matching dotfile link as it cleanly snaps and deletes it from existence.

Note: If your repository folder is named something else or is located somewhere else, simply change $HOME/dotfiles/* to match your actual path, keeping the wildcard asterisk * at the end.

Step 2: Manually Check .config (If applicable)

Many modern tools (like Starship, Alacritty, or Kitty) put their configurations inside ~/.config/. If your deployment script symlinked an entire folder instead of individual files, the previous step might leave that folder link behind.

Run this command to check for and remove any lingering directory symlinks in your config folder: 

find ~/.config -maxdepth 2 -type l -lname "$HOME/dotfiles/*" -verbose -delete

Step 3: Shred and Delete the Dotfiles Repository

Now that all the symlink bridges are gone, the configurations are completely unhooked from your system. You can now securely overwrite and delete the physical dotfiles repository so your custom scripts, notes, or comments can't be recovered.

  • On your Linux Workstation (using shred): 
    find ~/dotfiles -type f -exec shred -u -z -n 3 {} +
rm -rf ~/dotfiles
  • On your macOS Client (macOS does not natively have shred, use rm -P to overwrite): 
    rm -rfP ~/dotfiles

Step 4: The Ultimate Safety Check (Find Broken Symlinks)

To be absolutely certain that no orphaned, broken shortcuts are left behind cluttering up your home directory, run a final scan to display any dead links: 

find ~ -maxdepth 3 -type l ! -exec test -e {} \; -print

If this command returns any lines, those are "broken" links. If you see they are old dotfiles you missed, you can safely remove them manually using rm.

⚠️ A Crucial Final Note on Your Active Shell

Because you just deleted your .zshrc or .bashrc symlink, your current terminal window will keep running smoothly using the configuration it already loaded into RAM. However, do not close your current terminal window until you are fully finished cleaning the machine. If you close it, opening a new window will drop you into a completely bare, unconfigured system environment, making it much harder to run final commands.
